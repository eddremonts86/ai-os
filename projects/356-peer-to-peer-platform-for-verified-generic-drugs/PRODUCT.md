# Peer-to-peer platform for verified generic drugs

## Value Proposition

A peer-to-peer marketplace that turns "this strip might or might not be the drug it claims to be" into "this batch came from this licensed manufacturer, was checked by us, and is sold to you with a receipt that names the lot." The buyer gets a generic at a lower price than the brand and pays no more trust than they would pay a chemist; the seller gets a direct channel to buyers who currently avoid generics; the platform takes a small transaction fee on each verified sale.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Patients paying out-of-pocket in India | Pay the brand price even when a generic would do; need provenance they can trust. |
| Small licensed generic manufacturers and distributors | Have stock buyers reject; need a channel that proves origin. |
| Chemists and small pharmacies | Want verified lots on the shelf so the walk-in buyer does not push back. |
| Caregivers buying for a family member | Risk-averse; will pay more if the substitute is verified. |

## Jobs To Be Done

- "I have a brand strip / prescription and I want to find the verified generic equivalent without guessing."
- "I want to know, before I pay, that this batch is from a real factory and is within expiry."
- "I want to buy a small quantity of a generic I trust, not commit to a 30-strip pack from an unknown seller."
- "I run a small generic manufacturer / distributor and I want buyers to trust my stock without me having to build a brand."
- "I am a chemist, and I want to stock generics my customers will accept in place of the brand."

## Success Metrics

- A verified buyer purchases a verified generic lot and the receipt carries the batch number, manufacturer, and the seller's license ID.
- A buyer who starts on the brand-name product for a chronic condition switches to the verified generic and stays on it for the next refill cycle.
- Listings with completed license verification convert at a higher rate than listings without verification.
- Repeat purchases by the same buyer within 90 days, indicating the trust survived the first order.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Tata 1mg, PharmEasy, Netmeds, Apollo 24/7** — branded delivery pharmacies that sell generic substitutes but do not show per-batch provenance to the buyer.
- **Jan Aushadhi (PMBJP)** — government-affiliated generic stores that do sell at low prices but with a fixed retail footprint and limited online reach.
- **Direct manufacturer-to-pharmacy distribution** — the existing B2B channel that moves verified stock to chemists but does not surface the verification to the end buyer.

## Risks & Open Questions

- The platform's promise that "this is the drug it says it is" is load-bearing; any counterfeit that slips through verification destroys the category for the user — and one bad batch is more visible than a hundred good ones.
- Indian state drug controllers vary; a license that is valid in Maharashtra may not be honoured in Karnataka, and the platform MVP must decide whether to restrict to one state or carry that complexity.
- The buyer often arrives with a brand strip and wants a generic substitute; offering that convenience introduces a pharmacy / prescribing role that the MVP is not licensed to perform.
- A fee model that pays for the human verifier requires either high-ticket orders or volume; the first cohort of listings must be selected so the unit economics test honestly.
- The Manual source did not name a price, an MVP launch date, or a pilot city; the team must pick all three before the first verified transaction.
