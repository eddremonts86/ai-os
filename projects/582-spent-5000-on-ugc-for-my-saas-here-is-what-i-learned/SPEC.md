# UGC retrospective — what $5,000 of small-account creators taught one SaaS

## Problem

The poster is a SaaS founder who spent roughly $5,000 on user-generated content (UGC) and is sharing what worked and what didn't, framed as "what not to do". The source explains the David Park / Jenni AI playbook the author followed: find influencers with viral potential but small accounts (cheaper, supposedly similar results), pay them per 100k views, and let them post on new accounts. The result was content that "directly shilled the product which isn't usually good" — the partner creators posted low-quality material optimised to hit the per-view payment, not to go viral. The author's stated revised plan is the opposite strategy: start with larger creators, bundle them on a monthly multi-video cadence, and run small accounts as a warm-up funnel that graduates to targeted content.

## Objective

Document the failure modes of the small-account, pay-per-view UGC playbook, and surface the alternative strategy the author intends to try next. The author is not pitching a product — they are crowdsourcing feedback and shared learning from other founders who have run UGC.

## Target Users

SaaS founders running or considering a UGC campaign. The source implies the reader has heard the "UGC beats ads" thesis and is deciding whether to run it.

## MVP Scope

The source is a retrospective, not a product spec. The "MVP" in this sense is the comparison: the playbook the author tried (small accounts, paid per 100k views, new accounts) versus the playbook the author proposes next (larger creators, monthly bundles, small accounts as warm-up). The deliverable is the lesson itself, not a build.
