## Design direction

The product is a venue listing, not a marketplace. The visual register is editorial, not e-commerce: it should feel closer to a printed wedding magazine than to an Airbnb or a classifieds board. The goal is for an engaged couple to spend time on the page, not to scroll past it.

Three principles drive the screen:

- **Photo-led.** The first viewport is dominated by a wide hero image of the venue, not by a search bar. Capacity, location, and "request a visit" sit below the fold so the reader has time to form a first impression.
- **Bilingual, parallel.** Arabic and French live side by side on the profile; the reader chooses which language to read in, but the structure is the same in both. Pricing bands, capacity, and menu samples are presented in both.
- **Calm and unhurried.** The page accepts that weddings are planned months in advance. There is no countdown, no urgency banner, no "only 2 dates left this month." Pressure is not the conversion mechanic; clarity is.

The MVP surface is small: a venue profile, a directory index of three to five halls in the same city, and an inquiry form. There is no booking flow, no payment, no calendar widget that promises real-time availability.
