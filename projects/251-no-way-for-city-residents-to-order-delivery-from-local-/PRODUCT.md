# No way for city residents to order delivery from local stores to their elderly relatives in remote villages

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

A service that closes the city-to-village delivery gap: a city resident places an order from a phone or laptop, the order is matched to a local store near the elderly recipient, a courier reaches the village, and the buyer is notified when delivery is confirmed. The recipient does not need an app, an account, or internet access. The buyer does not need to be physically present.

## Target Users

- City residents with elderly relatives in remote Russian villages.
- Adult children who care for a parent from a distance and want a reliable, repeatable channel.
- Smaller village stores that would accept orders if order-taking and payment were handled for them.
- Local couriers (private drivers or motorbike riders) who would take on last-mile delivery if the orders arrived structured.

## Jobs To Be Done

- When my elderly parent in a village runs out of groceries, I want to order from a local store and have it delivered, so I do not have to drive out or rely on a neighbour.
- When my parent needs a specific over-the-counter medicine, I want a delivery channel that reaches the village, so they do not have to find a ride to the district pharmacy.
- When I am a village store owner, I want incoming orders that come with payment already taken, so I can fulfil them without chasing the buyer.
- When I am a courier in a village, I want a structured delivery job with a known pickup and drop-off, so the trip is worth the petrol.

## Success Metrics

- Number of villages served (active store + courier coverage).
- Successful deliveries per week per village (proxy for reliability).
- Average time from order placement to delivery confirmation.
- Repeat-buyer rate: percentage of buyers who place a second order within 60 days (proxy for the recurring "care from a distance" use case).

## Risks & Open Questions

- Validate problem with 5 buyer interviews before MVP: confirm that the city-to-village delivery gap matches what adult children of elderly villagers actually face, and that they would pay a sustainable rural delivery rate.
- Last-mile economics: a 30 km courier trip cannot be priced like a city delivery. The MVP must size the price the buyer is willing to pay against the cost of the courier.
- Store onboarding is manual: village stores do not have APIs. The MVP needs an operator who can onboard 20–30 villages by hand before the platform can grow on its own.
- Recipient safety: the recipient is offline and elderly. The MVP must not require them to handle payments, scan codes, or install anything. A delivery photo and a courtesy call are the right confirmation level.
- Medicine scope: over-the-counter items are in scope; prescription medicine requires pharmacist verification that the MVP does not provide until the regulatory path is clear.
