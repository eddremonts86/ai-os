# Hot take: PMF can overcompensate for marketing, but onboarding journeys are pre-requisite for them

## Problem

In a lot of posts lately in this community, I've seen that almost every SaaS founder (including me) have struggled in marketing at some point or another. As a solo tech co-founder with 150k+ users in my app and 4+ years of experience in building for a very niche market of event tech, here are some of my learning that could be helpful for fellow founders: There's been a growing narrative of Marketing is more important than building product - which is true for most part. But fundamentally I argue that in SaaS, Marketing and building should be looked upon as same thing rather than different. Here's what I mean: Every Founder should ask themselves: When a user signs up on their platform, at what step should they consider the "Value" from their SaaS being delivered? How many steps does it take for the user to reach that milestone? How far are users actually reach in this funnel? If there's a significant drop at any particular step, that's your signal that this step needs to be easier. Once you have maximum people who are able to see the value your platform is providing, only then they are at a stage to "Decide" whether your product is worth buying or not. Otherwise, they might be losing interest before even understanding what exact value your product offers. You could get 100 sign-ups but people 90% of them might get bored or lose interest because they didn't know what next to do at any step. (Sucks to be in a short attention economy really) Therefore, your product needs to be self-guiding such that even a very distracted person can understand the Value at almost no mindful effort. This post is already too long, but in a different post I'll share some good references that I have followed for a smooth onboarding experience. Without that, the fundamental analysis of whether you have a PMF completely fails, because people aren't even realizing what value your product adds to their life/business. Hope this helps! :) submitted by /u/viditmittal99 [link] [comments]

## Objective

The MVP delivers a self-service marketing-audit template that a solo SaaS founder runs against their own signup funnel to find the step where attention bleeds out before value is delivered. The source post is an opinion rather than a product, so the MVP respects that by framing the deliverable as a structured audit playbook a founder can execute on Monday morning rather than a SaaS subscription. The audit asks the four questions the post explicitly names — when does value arrive, how many steps to get there, how far do users actually reach, where does the funnel drop — and produces a written brief that names the step to fix first. The author positions their own experience at 150k+ users in a niche event-tech market over 4+ years as the credibility surface for the framework.

## Target Users

1. **Solo tech co-founders** who can build but struggle with marketing and want a checklist rather than a course.
2. **Early-stage SaaS founders** sitting on a signup funnel that converts poorly and looking for a concrete first step before hiring a growth marketer.
3. **Product managers at niche SaaS products** who need a written brief to align a small team around the onboarding step that is bleeding attention.
4. **Indie hackers and bootstrapped founders** who already believe PMF is good and need help proving or disproving it through funnel evidence.
5. **Niche-market SaaS founders** whose addressable audience is small and therefore cannot afford a leaky funnel — the cohort the source poster explicitly identifies with.

## MVP Scope

- A written audit playbook that walks a founder through the four questions the post names (value-arrival step, step count to value, actual reach, drop location) with worked examples for a SaaS signup funnel.
- A funnel-mapping worksheet the founder fills in by hand or in a spreadsheet, capturing the steps, the drop rate at each step, and the hypothesised cause.
- A "first step to fix" template that produces a one-page brief naming the highest-impact onboarding step and a concrete change to test.
- A library of onboarding patterns the poster alludes to in the post ("good references that I have followed for a smooth onboarding experience"), captured as a short annotated bibliography rather than a course.
- A landing page that frames the playbook as a marketing-audit template for solo and niche SaaS founders, with a single CTA to download the playbook.
- An optional companion template (a markdown document) that the founder can copy into their own repo or Notion workspace and start filling in immediately.

## Constraints

- The MVP will not ship as a SaaS subscription; the source is a Reddit opinion post and the appropriate response is a template, not a product.
- The MVP will not promise a course, cohort, or live coaching; the poster's credibility is a written playbook, not a programme.
- The MVP will not invent metrics or case studies the poster did not share; the only proof point in the source is the 150k+ user count and 4+ years in event-tech, and the MVP will reference those without adding fabricated numbers.
- The MVP will not duplicate the "references for smooth onboarding" the poster promises to share later; that body of work is a separate post and the MVP will link out rather than recreate.
- The MVP will not take a strong position on PMF versus marketing as a debate; it operationalises the post's "they are the same thing" claim into a four-question audit a founder can run.
