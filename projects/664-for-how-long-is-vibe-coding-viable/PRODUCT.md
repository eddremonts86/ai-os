# For how long is vibe coding viable?

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

A self-assessment tool that tells a vibe-coded project's founder whether the project is still viable, exhausted, or graduating beyond vibe-coding — and what to do this week in each case. The audit is opinionated and honest: it will sometimes tell the founder to stop. That is the wedge against generic "AI startup" content.

**One-liner:** Twelve questions, one verdict, three next actions.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Solo vibe-coder | Has shipped at least one app. Wants a reality test before the next month of work, not after. |
| Buyer / investor | Evaluating a vibe-coded project. Needs a structured audit to compare it against alternatives without doing the work themselves. |
| Career switcher | Entered SaaS via AI coding tools. Wants a stable rubric to decide whether to keep going. |
| Indie hacker with vibe-coded competitors | Wants to know whether the competitive landscape is going to collapse or stabilize, and where their project sits in that. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Produce a single verdict (still viable, exhausted, graduating) and a concrete next action the founder can take this week.
2. **Emotional job** — Replace "I have a feeling this isn't working" with a written verdict and a rubric the founder can argue with.
3. **Social job** — Give the founder a document they could show a peer, a co-founder candidate, or a buyer without having to defend the project from scratch.

## Success Metrics

- **Completion rate:** Percentage of users who finish all 12-15 questions in one sitting. Target: 60%, indicating the questionnaire is the right length.
- **Verdict distribution:** Track the share of audits that land in each of the three states. A roughly even split is healthy — if 95% land in "still viable" the rubric is not calibrated.
- **Next-action rate:** Percentage of users who report attempting at least one next action within 14 days. Target: 35%.
- **Audit-aged-well rate:** Percentage of founders who, 30 days after running the audit, agree the verdict still holds. Target: 70%.

## Competitive Landscape

- **AI-startup Twitter / LinkedIn advice threads** — generic motivation content. The MVP is a structured audit, not advice.
- **IndieHackers milestones / kill-the-project threads** — community signal. The MVP is per-project, not aggregated.
- **YC's "Default Alive / Default Dead" framework** — a related concept but oriented to startup financial runway, not vibe-coding viability.
- **AI-coding tool vendor content** — incentives to keep the founder using the tool. The MVP has no such incentive.
- **Founder mental-health and accountability communities** — adjacent in tone. The MVP is a tool, not a community.

## Risks & Open Questions

- Honest gap: the audit is opinionated, and some founders will disagree with the verdict. Mitigation: the rubric's logic is published in the audit's footer so the founder can argue with the tool, not just the result.
- The "exhausted" verdict is uncomfortable. Mitigation: the audit pairs every exhausted verdict with the most likely cause and a small set of recovery actions. It is a verdict, not a dismissal.
- The 30-day "did this age well" loop adds a small marketing surface. Mitigation: the reminder is optional, clearly opt-in, and the founder can turn it off.
- The "graduating" verdict may direct vibe-coders to hire developers they cannot afford. Mitigation: the next-action set in that state explicitly includes "stop spending on the project" as a valid option.
