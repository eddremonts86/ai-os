# Coming soon: validated problems for startups with a high chance of success. Our mission: 0% failed startups.

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

Stop betting a year on a problem that nobody has paid to solve. The first 100 founders who buy a validated problem get a refund if the listed problem turns out to have no paying customer within 12 months — if the platform is willing to put money behind the mission.

## Target Users

Solo founders, first-time founders, and corporate innovation teams who are tired of pitching ideas that turn out to have no buyer. Also investors scouting the platform as a deal-flow source.

## Jobs To Be Done

Functional: pick a problem that already has demand. Emotional: stop the imposter feeling of working on something nobody wants. Social: be the founder who came to investor calls with a problem the platform had already validated.

## Success Metrics

At least 40% of founders who buy a validated problem from the platform build something that ships. At least 20% of those projects reach paying customers within 12 months. Public methodology is cited by external analysts at least once per quarter.

## Competitive Landscape

Existing startup idea directories (IdeaBrowse, Springwise) publish problems without validation. Reddit and Indie Hackers surface demand signals but do not score them. The platform's claim is methodological, not a feature, so the bar is whether the methodology survives a competent review.

## Risks & Open Questions

The '0% failed startups' target is unattainable in any reasonable definition of failure; the platform is on the hook to define failure publicly. Analysts must be unbiased — accepting sponsorship from a submitter would corrupt the score. Founders may treat a passing score as a license to skip their own customer interviews.
