# Does making TikTok videos about your B2C SaaS work?

## Problem

The poster launched their B2C SaaS a year ago and has made around $60 with it. All users came organically, no paid ads, with a decent SEO but tough competitors. The question is whether starting to make videos and posting on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube is worth it. The poster is stressed about putting their face online and is asking peers whether the bet is real. The post is a question, not a complaint. No country, no competitor, no install count was stated.

## Objective

Give a B2C SaaS founder who is considering a TikTok / Reels / Shorts motion a realistic range of what the channel returns for a B2C SaaS with a small organic base, with the named time cost and the named face-on-camera tax. The job is not to make the videos — it is to let the founder decide whether the channel is worth the discomfort before they commit a quarter.

## Target Users

Primary: a B2C SaaS founder (planner, journal, learning, habit, fitness, productivity) with a small organic base who is being told to start TikTok and is weighing the face-on-camera tax. Secondary: a first marketing hire at the same kind of company who is being asked to launch the video channel and is sizing the return.

## MVP Scope

In scope for v1:

- A 'does it work for B2C SaaS' verdict: the named range, with the named SEO baseline the poster has as the comparison.
- A face-on-camera tax section: the named time and discomfort cost, with the named alternatives (faceless formats, screen recordings, voice-only).
- A channel-shape sketch: what the first 90 days of TikTok / Reels / Shorts look like for a B2C SaaS with a small organic base.
- A one-page export the founder can drop into a Notion doc or a Monday morning standup.

**Color** — neutral surface (off-white / off-black per OS theme), one accent for primary actions, one muted accent for secondary. No gradients in v1.

**Type** — one display family for headings, one text family for body, one mono for code/numbers. Type scale is small (4 steps) so the layout stays compact.

**Density** — tight, table-driven for dashboards; generous spacing for content-heavy screens.

**Motion** — minimal: page transitions only when the user explicitly navigates. No autoplay, no parallax.

## Constraints

- The source is a question, not a feature request — the deliverable is a verdict, not a product.
- No country, no install count, no revenue was stated; the MVP must work for any B2C SaaS with a small organic base.
- The output must not invent a viral-hit rate — name the channel and let the founder fill in the number from their own experiment.
