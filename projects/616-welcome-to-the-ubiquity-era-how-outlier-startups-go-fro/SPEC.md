# Welcome to the Ubiquity Era: How Outlier Startups Go from $0 to $100M by Becoming Everywhere

## Problem
 I’ve spent 12 years working in B2B SaaS, and I think founders dramatically overestimate how much comparison shopping buyers actually do. Most buyers are not running deep diligence or evaluating 30 products over 180 days. No... they simply buy from the tiny handful of names already in their head when the problem becomes urgent or when the market tips. If you aren’t one of those names, you're SOL. This will become much more apparent over the next decade. Break out AI companies find ways to get carried into the market fast and hard: Figma turned every shared design into distribution Calendly put its name inside every meeting invitation HubSpot taught the category until its language became the category’s language Clay built an army of creators, templates and visible customer workflows Cursor entered developer conversation so quickly that it began feeling like the default It's much more than “brand awareness.” That's too vague - it’s product distribution, public artifacts, community, founder media and repeated exposure reinforcing one another. Eventually a company crosses a line where people recommend it before they’ve bought it / talent wants in / partners pursue it / existing users make the company more visible simply by using it. The market starts doing part of the distribution for you. The uncomfortable implication is that a superior product can remain invisible for years while another wins because it is easier to remember, easier to share and already present inside the buyer’s peer group. I’m building a YouTube series to reverse-engineer how companies reach that point. Disclosure: I made the video and the channel is mine. No product pitch or email gate. My five bets among 2026-born AI companies already showing the first signs of breakout familiarity: AMI Labs, Ineffable Intelligence, River AI, Discovery Loop and Core Automation.
