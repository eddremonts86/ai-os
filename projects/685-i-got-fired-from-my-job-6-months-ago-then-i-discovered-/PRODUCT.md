# I got fired from my job 6 months ago. Then I discovered a problem I couldn't stop thinking about.

> Product brief for the phone-distraction interruption layer scoped in the source post.

## Value Proposition

A solo founder or knowledge worker can install a single-purpose guard rail that surfaces a deliberate pause before opening a distracting app — without subscribing to a screen-time suite or installing a focus app they will abandon in a week.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Solo founders and knowledge workers | Have noticed their own screen-time spiral; want friction that does not require willpower. |
| Students and creators | Treat the phone as a deliberate tool; want a guard rail. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Pause before opening a distracting app and decide whether the open is deliberate.
2. **Emotional job** — Stop feeling ambushed by the phone.
3. **Social job** — None.

## Success Metrics

- **Activation:** first deliberate-pause tap within 7 days of install.
- **Retention:** at least 5 deliberate pauses/week per active user after month 1.
- **Self-reported screen-time reduction:** ≥ 30% within 30 days.

## Pricing & Monetization

Free tier: 3 apps in the list. Paid at $4.99/month or $29.99/year: unlimited apps, daily-tally analytics.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Opal** — full focus suite, more expensive.
- **One Sec** — pre-open delay, similar idea.
- **ScreenZen** — focus mode with scheduling.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] The iOS Screen Time API limits what the app can do; the MVP must be small enough to fit inside those limits.
- [ ] The product is a single-purpose guard rail; the broader focus-suite competitors may out-features it.
