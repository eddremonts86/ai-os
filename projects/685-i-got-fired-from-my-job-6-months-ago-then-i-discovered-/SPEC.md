# I got fired from my job 6 months ago. Then I discovered a problem I couldn't stop thinking about.

## Problem
 I wasn't sad when I got fired. I actually thought: “Maybe this is finally my chance to build something online.” So I started building apps. And I learned a painful lesson pretty quickly: Building something is easy. Building something people actually care about is not. I'd spend days making a product, launch it, and realize I was basically the only person who wanted it. After a while, I lost motivation. And that's when I started wasting a ridiculous amount of time on my phone. Reels. Shorts. Random videos. My screen time reached 8–9 hours a day. But the number wasn't even the part that bothered me. It was realizing that I wasn't consciously choosing most of that time. I'd unlock my phone to do one thing, open Instagram without thinking, and suddenly 40 minutes were gone. So I started looking at the problem differently. I'm a developer. Why was I trying to beat software designed to capture my attention with nothing but willpower? Why not use software against the behavior itself? So I built something for myself. I called it Pausee. The idea isn't to block distracting apps. It's to interrupt the automatic decision to open them. Before a distracting app opens, Pausee adds a short pause and asks me, essentially: “Did you actually choose this?” I added a few things around that idea too: A pause before opening distracting apps A scroll stopper for long sessions Usage windows for intentional sessions Mindfulness prompts before continuing And the result surprised me. My screen time went from 8–9 hours to around 3–4 hours. It isn't perfect. I still use social media and I still waste time. The difference is that I'm starting to notice the behavior before it happens. I'm keeping Pausee completely free because I built it to solve a problem I genuinely had, not to put another paywall in front of digital wellbeing. Now I'm interested in the business lesson behind it: How do you know when a problem you personally care about is actually a problem other people care enough about? I'm still trying to figure that out.
