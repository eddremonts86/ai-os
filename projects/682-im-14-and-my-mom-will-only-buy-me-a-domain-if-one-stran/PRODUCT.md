# I'm 14 and my mom will only buy me a domain if one stranger actually pays for my app.

> Product brief for the essay keystroke-replay verifier described in the source post.

## Value Proposition

A student can produce a tamper-evident replay of every keystroke and paste on an essay, share it with a teacher as a single link, and walk away with proof of authorship that Google Docs history does not provide.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Students | Want proof of authorship after an AI-accusation incident. |
| Teachers | Want a verifiable artifact before honour-code enforcement. |
| Parents | Want to confirm their child's work is original. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Produce a tamper-evident replay of an essay's keystrokes.
2. **Emotional job** — Stop feeling accused with no way to defend.
3. **Social job** — Look diligent in front of teachers and parents.

## Success Metrics

- **Activation:** first essay recorded and replay link generated within 7 days of install.
- **Retention:** at least 3 essays recorded per active student per semester.
- **Viral coefficient:** at least 1 teacher introduced per 10 students via the replay link.

## Pricing & Monetization

Free tier: 5 essays/month, 720p replay. Paid at $4.99/month or $29.99/year: unlimited essays, full-fidelity replay, teacher-team sharing.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Google Docs history** — shows snapshots, not keystrokes.
- **Turnitin / GPTZero** — detection, not authorship proof.
- **Proctoring tools** — built for exams, not essays.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] The "25 verification methods" claim is a founder-claim; the MVP scopes 5-7 for shippability.
- [ ] The product's viral loop depends on teachers accepting the replay link as evidence; this is a behavioural shift, not a feature.
- [ ] The founder is 14; the product must not depend on their personal availability.
