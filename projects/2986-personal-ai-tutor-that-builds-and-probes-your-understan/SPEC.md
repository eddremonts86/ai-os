# Personal AI tutor that builds and probes your understanding

## Problem

The author used to learn from books and courses and kept noticing they hadn't built a real mental model — they needed an LLM on the side to fill the gaps the books skipped. So they stopped treating books as the source of truth and started using an LLM itself: design the curriculum, then probe whether they actually understood each chapter. The result is **Suki**, an open-source tool that, given a topic, builds a curriculum from beginner to advanced and quizzes the learner chapter by chapter to surface what they've actually retained.

## Objective

Ship an open-source, LLM-driven tutoring loop that turns any topic into a structured curriculum and a probing Q&A session, so the learner can tell whether they've built a real mental model or just skimmed a textbook. Keep it open source and CLI-friendly so the workflow integrates with how developers already work.

## Target Users

1. **Self-taught developers and students** moving through a new domain who keep finding books leave gaps and want a structured + probed learning loop.
2. **Engineers pivoting into a new stack** who want a curriculum they can drill through with probing instead of bouncing between blog posts.
3. **Tutors and educators** who want a free, open-source framework to scaffold personalised study plans for their students.

## MVP Scope

- CLI input: a topic string.
- Output: a chapter-by-chapter curriculum generated by an LLM, beginner to advanced.
- Per-chapter probing Q&A loop that adapts to what the learner actually understood.
- Persistent memory of what the learner has covered, so re-runs pick up where they left off.
- Open-source (GitHub) with a clean install path; no paid tier required to use.

## Constraints

- The source post does not state a price. Suki is open source per the post; pricing is left as an open question (hosted tier? team plans? donations?).
- Threat model is local-first: the learner owns their study data.
- Quality of probing depends on the LLM used; a hosted backend is not implied by the source and is out of scope unless the author chooses to add one.
