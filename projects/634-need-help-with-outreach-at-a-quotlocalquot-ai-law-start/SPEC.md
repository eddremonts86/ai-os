# need help with outreach at a "local" AI law startup (i will not promote)

## Problem
 I am the founder of a central European AI lawyer platform. Right now all the leads come from Google SEO, people searching "lawyer AI" (in local language) When i made the SaaS it was very basic, almost like a PoC, unusable for real lawyers, however at the time i didnt care, i posted it on local reddit ("young local founder made AI law startup") and it gained a lot of traction and even got me an article at a local news site, which pulled a lot of traffic, but ever since then the traffic died down (obviously the product was bad). The obvious answer is to just make a reddit post bu the original one highlighted how I was a young founder and that it is a free project and now we became a bit more corporate. It's still doable and we offer free tiers, but I am putting it off. Emailing our previous users, already on the table and doing in waves. People are coming over. Other than that it's just the SEO, so I'm looking for some way to turn a dial and throw money at it and get some traffic of actual lawyers or practicioners that would be power users and work with documents a lot, I'm not expecting miracles just experiment with it and hopefully make some money. What would be the best way to reach them? Google ads? (I've never used Google Ads, do I have to select keywords or something?), Facebook ads?, LinkedIn? submitted by /u/seruZ12

## Objective

Produce a concrete outreach playbook for a central European legal-tech founder who has SEO humming but no paid distribution, has burned through a one-time Reddit + news-article spike, and is hesitating to repeat the founder-story trick now that the company is more corporate. The playbook combines the channel the founder already has (local-language SEO on "lawyer AI"), a paid acquisition experiment scoped to lawyers and practitioners who would actually use the product on documents, and a small CRM to keep the re-engagement emails already happening from falling on the floor. The deliverable is a documented runbook plus a working dashboard that the founder can use to decide whether to spend on Google Ads, LinkedIn, or Facebook after a two-week test.

## Target Users

- **The central European AI-law founder** (SeruZ12) — has SEO working, has one shot of PR-style virality behind them, has prior users to re-engage, and would spend money on ads if they had a clear targeting story.
- **Solo or two-person legal-tech founder** in another CEE market (Poland, Czechia, Hungary, Romania) who is in the same position: local SEO works, paid acquisition is a black box.
- **Practising lawyers and paralegals** who are the buyers and power users — they care about document-heavy workflows specifically, not generic AI for law.
- **Local bar associations and small firm owners** — the channel through which practitioners trust new tools, and a paid placement that fits.
- **Outsourced marketing freelancer or agency** the founder might hire to execute the playbook once it is documented.

## MVP Scope

- A channel-by-channel outreach runbook for the legal-tech vertical: SEO (defensive), Google Ads (search + keywords), LinkedIn (sponsored content + InMail), Facebook (retargeting only), bar-association sponsorship, and direct cold email.
- A paid-ads experiment scaffold: three Google Ads campaigns, each with a small daily budget, three keyword groups matched to "lawyer AI in local language" variants, and a two-week measurement window.
- A lightweight CRM table for re-engagement: previous users, stage (contacted, replied, signed up, churned), and a templated email sequence the founder can send in waves.
- A targeting brief document: which lawyer segments to pursue (litigation, contract review, doc-heavy back-office) and which signals to use (firm size, practice area, language).
- A landing-page variant for each paid channel so the founder can attribute which ad drove which law firm.
- A weekly scorecard template (spend, signups, activated lawyers, retained firms) that the founder can fill in by hand.

## Constraints

- All paid channels must be runnable by a non-marketing founder; no agency-required setup.
- The playbook is country-specific to a CEE local-language market; English-language global markets are out of scope.
- Compliance with the local bar's advertising rules is preserved — the playbook does not suggest copy that would violate bar advertising ethics.
- The MVP does not include sending cold emails at scale; the founder does individual outreach already, and the product documents that loop, not automates it.
- No fake urgency, no "AI replacement for lawyers" marketing — the founder's audience is lawyers, who would mistrust that copy instantly.
