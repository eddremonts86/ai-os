# How do you know when a SaaS company has outgrown spreadsheets for partner management?

> Auto-generated product brief.

## Value Proposition

A diagnostic for first-time SaaS founders who are being told they need a partner operations tool and want to know whether they actually have crossed the threshold or whether they are about to buy a tool they don't yet need. Generic SaaS content in this space is mostly vendor marketing. This one answers the question the post is actually asking: what is the named signal that the spreadsheet has stopped working?

## Target Users

Primary: a SaaS founder or first sales hire at a Series A or earlier company who has been running partners out of a spreadsheet and is being told they need a PRM. Secondary: the head of sales or partnerships at a slightly larger SaaS company (post-Series A) who is sizing the move from the spreadsheet to a real PRM and wants to know what the trigger looks like.

## Jobs To Be Done

When I (a SaaS founder or first sales hire) am being told by my board or my peers that I need a PRM or partner CRM, give me a concrete diagnostic — the first thing that breaks and the 5-question scorecard — so I can decide whether I am at the trigger or whether I am about to spend on a tool that solves a problem I haven't hit yet.

## Success Metrics

1. The poster (or a comparable founder) can name, after one read, the first thing that broke in their spreadsheet that justified the move to a PRM.
2. A founder can answer the scorecard against their own reality and get a verdict (trigger, getting close, or stay).
3. The vendor-research time the founder spends after reading the output is shorter than the time they would have spent cold.

## Risks & Open Questions

- The source is a question, not a complaint; the MVP succeeds only if the diagnostic actually changes the founder's decision, not if they read it politely.
- The answer 'stay with the spreadsheet' is a real outcome, and the MVP must say it out loud when it is true. A diagnostic that always pushes the founder toward buying is not a diagnostic.
- One founder is not a market. The named triggers must show up in 3+ comparable companies or the plan is solving a one-off, not a recurring pain.
- The line between CRM and PRM is fuzzy by design (most CRMs now have partner modules). The MVP must respect that fuzziness rather than pretending it is a clean boundary.
