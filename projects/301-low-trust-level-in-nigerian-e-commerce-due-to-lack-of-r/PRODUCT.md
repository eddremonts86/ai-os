# Low trust level in Nigerian e-commerce due to lack of reliable logistics service

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

A Nigerian merchant can promise a delivery window that buyers can actually verify, with a single status page that follows the package from pickup to drop-off and a clear path to dispute if something goes wrong.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Small Nigerian e-commerce merchant | Currently loses orders because buyers do not trust the delivery; needs a logistics brand they can put on the order confirmation. |
| Nigerian online buyer (COD or prepaid) | Cannot verify the order is moving; needs a status page and a phone number to reach. |
| Independent dispatch rider | Needs a steady feed of jobs with same-day or next-day payouts, not a 30-day wait. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Get a parcel from the merchant to the buyer with proof of delivery and a clear status trail.
2. **Emotional job** — Feel safe paying online or on delivery because there is a real company behind the last mile.
3. **Social job** — Be able to share a tracking link in a WhatsApp chat instead of just promising "it will arrive".

## Success Metrics

- **On-time delivery rate** ≥ 90% within the merchant's chosen city, measured against the promised window.
- **Dispute resolution time** median ≤ 48 hours from buyer filing to merchant payout freeze lifted.
- **Rider utilisation** ≥ 4 deliveries per active rider per day in pilot cities.
- **Repeat merchant usage** ≥ 60% of merchants place a second order within 14 days of the first delivery.

## Pricing & Monetization

Per-parcel fee (Naira) + a small percentage of COD amount held until reconciliation completes. Subscription tier for merchants shipping > 200 parcels per month with predictable volume.

## Competitive Landscape

- **GIG Logistics, DHL, FedEx** — international couriers with Nigerian offices; price and documentation overhead exclude small merchants.
- **Local dispatch collectives (Kwik, Delly)** — informal rider networks with no merchant-facing dashboard and no dispute flow.
- **In-house rider teams** — large marketplaces run their own fleets, but a standalone merchant cannot buy into them.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] Validate the 48-hour dispute median with a Lagos pilot before charging for the service.
- [ ] Confirm whether Termii's delivery receipt API can replace manual SMS pings without raising delivery cost.
- [ ] Decide on BVN storage policy — needed for rider payouts, but a privacy burden if mis-handled.
