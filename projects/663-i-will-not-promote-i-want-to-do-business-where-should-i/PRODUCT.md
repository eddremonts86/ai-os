# I will not promote: I want to do business. Where should I start?

> Auto-generated product brief. Reviewed and refined for the first-time founder onboarding template.

## Value Proposition

The poster is a first-time founder with a business background but no operating experience, asking seven questions at once: how to know if an idea is worth pursuing, how important the idea is, whether to enter a new field, whether to copy an existing model, how different a copy needs to be, what mistakes to avoid, and what books to read. The post is a question, not a product surface, and the honest answer is a structured 30-day playbook plus a private decision journal that walks the founder through each of the seven questions in order. The founder ends the 30 days with a scored idea, a logged set of problem interviews, a written distribution hypothesis, and a one-page rationale they can defend to a peer. The product is the operating manual the poster wishes existed; it is generic on purpose because the source provides no specific vertical.

## Target Users

- **First-time founder with a business degree but no operating experience** — cares because they have theory but no script, and the playbook is the script.
- **Career-changer considering a first business** — cares because they have savings runway but no direction, and the playbook forces a decision in 30 days.
- **Solo founder-to-be with a list of half-formed ideas** — cares because the idea scorecard lets them compare candidates on the same rubric.
- **University student or recent graduate** considering entrepreneurship — cares because the playbook is a 30-day trial that fits in a summer.
- **Small-business adviser or mentor** running a first-time-founder cohort — cares because the workbook is the structured handout they would otherwise write by hand.

## Jobs To Be Done

- **Functional:** When I want to start a business but have no idea where to begin, I want a 30-day playbook with a decision journal, so I can move from "stuck at zero" to "I have a scored idea and a written rationale" in one month.
- **Emotional:** I want to stop feeling like the only person who does not know how to start, so I can move from anxiety to operating.
- **Social:** I want to be able to tell a peer "I have a plan, here it is", so the conversation moves from "you should…" to "have you considered…".

## Success Metrics

- Fraction of founders who complete the 30-day playbook and end with a written decision (build / study / shelve) — target over 60%.
- Median number of problem interviews logged by the end of week 2 — target over 10.
- Fraction of founders who re-run the exercise six months later — target over 20%, because re-running is the signal of serious intent.
- Self-reported confidence score (1-10) at day 0 vs day 30 — target improvement of 2 points.
- Open rate of the weekly check-in email — target over 50%, because the check-in is the forcing function.

## Competitive Landscape

- **The Mom Test / Lean Startup / YC's Startup School** are books and courses; the product is a 30-day running playbook with a journal, not a course.
- **Notion templates for founder onboarding** are common but static; the weekly check-in is the live element the templates do not have.
- **r/startups threads** are the source the poster is asking, and the answer they get is uneven; the playbook is the consolidated answer.
- **Indie Hackers / MicroAcquire** are post-idea communities; the product is the pre-idea, pre-build structured walk.

## Risks & Open Questions

- The source is a Reddit question, not a product surface; the playbook is a generic founder-onboarding template, and the risk is that the founder needs a vertical-specific idea-lab, not a generic one.
- Will the 30-day window be too short for a founder who needs to validate a deep, vertical-specific idea, or too long for a founder who already has a candidate?
- Will the rubric be perceived as oversimplifying the "is my idea good?" question, and how does the playbook acknowledge that the rubric is a heuristic, not a verdict?
- The poster's seven questions are listed in one paragraph; the playbook orders them. The ordering is a design choice, and the founder may disagree, which is fine but should be flagged.
- The playbook presumes the founder has 30 days of part-time availability; many first-time founders do not, and the product must say so plainly.
