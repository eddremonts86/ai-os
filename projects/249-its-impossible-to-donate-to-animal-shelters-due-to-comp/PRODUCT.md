# It's impossible to donate to animal shelters due to complete distrust in charity fundraisers. There is no service with guaranteed transparency and audit

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

A donation platform where each ruble the donor gives is held by the service, released against shelter-submitted receipts, and confirmed by an independent monthly audit. The donor sees, on an ongoing basis, exactly which line item their contribution covered and what evidence supports it. Distrust is not solved by promises; it is solved by a public, auditable chain.

## Target Users

- Russian donors who want to support animal welfare but currently refuse to donate because of distrust in fundraisers.
- Past donors who have stopped giving after losing confidence.
- Shelter operators willing to accept third-party audits in exchange for more stable funding.
- Independent journalists and charity watchdogs who would use the audit trail as a public accountability surface.

## Jobs To Be Done

- When I want to give 1,000₽ to a shelter this month, I want to pick a specific line item (feed, vet, utilities) and see later exactly what was bought, so my gift is not a leap of faith.
- When a shelter asks me to give monthly, I want a monthly audit report I can read, so I can keep giving with confidence instead of stopping after one bad year.
- When I am a shelter operator and my funding is fragile, I want a steady donor base that trusts me, so I can plan beyond the next collection drive.
- When I am a journalist writing about charity fraud, I want a public ledger I can cite, so my story has evidence instead of hearsay.

## Success Metrics

- Number of recurring donors (monthly or quarterly) per partner shelter at month six (proxy for trust holding over time).
- Share of donated rubles backed by audit-confirmed receipts in the trailing 90 days.
- Donor retention: percentage of first-time donors who make a second donation within 90 days.
- Number of partner shelters on the platform (capped by the MVP's audit capacity, not an open marketplace).

## Competitive Landscape

## Risks & Open Questions

- Validate problem with 5 donor interviews before MVP: confirm that the distrust framing matches what Russian would-be donors actually say, and that an audit trail would change their behavior.
- Audit independence is hard to scale. The MVP depends on a contracted auditor; if the auditor becomes a bottleneck, the model breaks. Auditor capacity must be sized before shelter count grows.
- Shelter-side friction: receipts vary in quality, and a shelter with poor bookkeeping cannot pass the audit even if its work is honest. The MVP needs a lightweight receipt-helper (templates, photo-to-receipt guidance) for shelters that lack back-office staff.
- Money-holding regulatory scope: the service holds donor funds before disbursement. Russian charity law and payment regulation around this need to be checked before the MVP takes real money.
- Avoid drifting into a generic crowdfunding platform. The audit is the product; broadening to non-audited campaigns dilutes the trust signal.
