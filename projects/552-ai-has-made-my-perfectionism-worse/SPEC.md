# AI has made my perfectionism worse

## Problem
 Today I looked at my Git history and felt a bit stupid. From 2:02 to 7:07 PM, I made 18 commits around one progress indicator in my SaaS. At first, I only wanted users to see what their AI task was doing. Then I fixed auto-refresh, a race condition, polling ownership, and resume behavior. By the end, it had touched 13 files and added about 1,600 lines of code and tests. To be fair, some of those fixes were necessary. That is why it took me so long to notice what I was doing. As an engineer, perfectionism rarely feels like perfectionism. It feels like being responsible. There is always one more edge case that seems worth fixing. AI coding tools have made this worse for me. Another spec, test, or refactor feels cheap. My Git history looks productive: 260 commits in two days. But users do not care how busy my repo looks. They still saw one progress indicator. I think I was choosing the part of building where I felt in control. Code gives me an answer immediately. Shipping to real people might bring criticism, or worse, nothing at all. So I am trying a simpler rule: if it is safe, works, and users can understand it, ship it. More polish should be justified by what users actually do. How do you know when you are being a responsible engineer and when you are just hiding inside the code?
