# Users of credit products regularly overpay due to non-transparent bank terms. There is no advocate service that would analyze their spending and find hidden fees.

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

An independent, customer-side advocate that reads a Russian consumer's bank statement and credit agreement and produces a plain-language list of non-transparent fees, buried charges, and clauses that drive up the effective cost of their credit. The customer leaves with an evidence-backed report they can use to dispute a charge, negotiate a better tariff, or decide to refinance — without having to parse dense bank paperwork themselves.

## Target Users

- Russian adults with at least one active credit product (card with overdraft, consumer loan, installment plan, mortgage) who suspect hidden costs but cannot prove them.
- Customers about to sign a new credit agreement who want a second opinion on the fine print.
- Financially literate users who want a periodic audit (every 6–12 months) of their bank's tariff changes.
- Relatives helping an older family member audit an existing banking relationship.

## Jobs To Be Done

- When I open my bank's mobile app and see charges I don't recognize, I want a report that names each charge and explains it in plain Russian, so I can decide whether to dispute it.
- When I receive a new credit agreement, I want a quick second opinion on the effective cost, so I can compare offers before signing.
- When my bank changes its tariff, I want to be told what changed and how it affects my monthly cost, so I am not silently overcharged.
- When I help a parent audit their banking, I want a PDF I can show them and the bank, so the conversation has evidence instead of opinion.

## Success Metrics

- Number of Advocate Reports generated per week (proxy for organic demand and for retention if repeat usage is measured).
- Percentage of generated reports that flag at least one previously-unrecognized charge (proxy for the service's usefulness; the poster's framing implies this should usually be non-zero).
- Percentage of customers who return for a second report within 12 months (proxy for the "periodic audit" use case).
- Self-reported ruble amount of fees identified across all reports per month (proxy for customer value; the post does not state a baseline, so this is a measurement, not a target).

## Risks & Open Questions

- Validate problem with 5 user interviews before MVP: confirm that the stated lack of advocacy matches what Russian credit-product users actually experience, and that they would upload a statement to a third party.
- Confirm willingness to pay: the source does not state a price, so any pricing claim is a hypothesis to test, not a known fact.
- Define compliance scope: handling of personal financial data (statements, contracts) in Russia is regulated (Federal Law 152-FZ on personal data, plus banking secrecy under Federal Law 395-1). The MVP must specify data retention, deletion on request, and whether the service operates as a personal-data operator.
- Bank-statement format variety: Russian online-banking portals export statements in different formats (PDF, CSV, XLS). Coverage must be measured, not assumed.
- Avoid drifting into legal advice. Findings stop at "this is what the contract and statement show"; dispute strategy is out of scope for the MVP.
