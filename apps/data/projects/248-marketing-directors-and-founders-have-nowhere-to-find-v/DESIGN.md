## Design direction

The directory's whole purpose is trust. Every screen must answer one question for the buyer: *is this person real, and can I verify them before I reach out?*

Three principles drive the screen:

- **Verification status is the first thing the eye lands on.** The "verified" badge sits at the top of each profile, next to the name, not at the bottom. Unverified profiles are still listed (to keep the directory honest about its curation), but the badge is always visible.
- **References are reachable, not decorative.** Each reference shows a name and a one-line description; the buyer can request the contact. Hiding references behind a paywall would defeat the verification signal.
- **Outcome claims come with evidence.** A "3x ROAS in fintech" case is shown as a tile with the claim, the period, the account vertical, and a link to the evidence file (a screenshot, a redacted ad-account export) the buyer can request. Numbers without evidence do not appear on verified profiles.

The MVP surface is functional, not flashy: a directory index, a profile page, a contractor console, an operator console. No public-facing marketing pages, no blog, no decorative motion.
