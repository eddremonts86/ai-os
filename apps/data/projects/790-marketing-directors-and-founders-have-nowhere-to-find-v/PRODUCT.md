# Marketing directors and founders have nowhere to find verified contractors (targeting specialists, SMM managers) — the existing search process is a lottery that wastes time and budget

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

A two-niche directory of pre-vetted marketing contractors in Russia — targeting specialists and SMM managers — where a marketing director or founder can hire one in days, with verification that means the same thing to every buyer because every profile passed the same documented check.

The directory is narrow on purpose. Two specialties, one verification rubric per specialty, public profiles so buyers can browse without registering, and an inquiry flow that captures the outcome of every match so the directory learns which specialists actually close work. Verification is not a star count; it is a documented pass of a stated rubric, and the rubric is published so buyers know exactly what the badge means.

**One-liner:** A curated directory of pre-vetted targeting specialists and SMM managers in Russia, where the verification badge means a documented check has passed, not that the specialist rated themselves five stars.

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Marketing directors at small/mid Russian companies | Need a targeting specialist or SMM manager for a campaign and have no in-house recruiting pipeline. |

| Independent targeting specialists in Russia | Want a channel that surfaces their work to verified buyers rather than a generic freelance marketplace. |
| Independent SMM managers in Russia | Same: want a channel where their case studies are visible rather than buried. |
| Small Russian marketing agencies | Need a vetted bench of specialists to pull from for overflow work. |
| Returning buyers | Come back because repeat use is the trust signal the directory is meant to earn over time. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Find a vetted targeting specialist in a specific city, language and rate band, without reading twenty freelancer profiles to guess who can deliver.
2. **Functional job** — Find an SMM manager who has actually run a community of the size and topic the founder needs, not someone whose profile lists SMM as one skill among twenty.
3. **Functional job** — Open a private conversation with a shortlist of specialists at once, rather than messaging each from a personal email and losing the thread.
4. **Emotional job** — Stop the feeling that hiring a marketing contractor is a lottery every time.
5. **Social job** — Be the marketing director who hired well and can show the team which specialist was chosen and why.

## Success Metrics

- **Time-to-shortlist** — median minutes from first search to a buyer saving their first specialist. The source names wasted time as the pain; this is the direct inverse.
- **Hire rate** — share of inquiries that convert to a confirmed hire, captured via the outcome field on each inquiry. This is the directory's calibration signal.
- **Verification pass rate** — share of applicants who pass the published rubric on first attempt, which tells the directory whether the rubric is too loose or too strict.
- **Repeat-buyer rate** — share of buyers who run a second search within 90 days. The directory's value compounds only if buyers come back.
- **Specialist response rate** — share of inquiries a specialist replies to within 48 hours, which is the operational signal that the directory's matching is producing real conversations.
- **Niche mix** — share of searches by niche (targeting vs SMM), which tells the directory whether the two-lane scope is the right shape or one lane is dominant.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] Define the per-niche verification rubric so concretely that two reviewers would agree on the same pass-or-fail outcome for the same applicant.
- [ ] Confirm the inquiry-flow outcome capture does not become a chore buyers skip, which would starve the calibration signal.
- [ ] Decide whether the directory ever lists a specialist who failed the rubric (e.g. as an unverified entry), since hiding them entirely shrinks the pool but listing them confuses the badge.
- [ ] Decide how the directory handles a specialist whose verification later expires (a portfolio that ages, a reference who stops responding): badge state must be time-bound, not permanent.
- [ ] Validate with five Russian marketing directors that the two-niche scope (targeting + SMM) matches what they actually hire for, or whether the scope is too narrow.
- [ ] Confirm Russian-language copy alone is sufficient for the MVP, or whether a buyer-facing English surface is needed for specialists who serve bilingual clients.
