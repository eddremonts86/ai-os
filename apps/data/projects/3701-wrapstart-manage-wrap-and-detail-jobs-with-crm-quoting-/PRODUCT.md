# Wrapstart – Manage wrap and detail jobs with CRM, quoting, scheduling, and AI

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

One screen for a wrap, PPF, tint, ceramic or detailing shop: the call that came in while the owner was mid-install arrives as a transcribed lead with a follow-up already queued, the quote is built from the shop's own rates, the booked job lands on a named bay and tech, and the invoice settles through Stripe or Square and lands in QuickBooks. Profitability is visible per job while there is still time to change what gets booked.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Shop owner-operator | Answers the phone, writes the quotes and installs the film. Every call missed during an install is a job lost, and the loss is invisible because there is no record of it. |
| Installer / tech | Needs the day's assignment and the current job stage on a phone, in a bay, without leaving the work. |
| Front-desk or shop manager (larger shops) | Owns scheduling against bay and tech capacity, and takes the blame when two jobs need the same lift. |
| Bookkeeper / accountant | Wants invoices and payments already reconciled in QuickBooks instead of rebuilt from card-reader exports. |
| Customer booking a wrap | Wants to approve a quote, see whether the car is ready, and pay without a phone call. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Turn an inbound call into a booked, scheduled, invoiced job without retyping the same customer and vehicle into four tools.
2. **Emotional job** — Stop the specific dread of hearing the phone ring with hands on a panel and knowing that lead is gone.
3. **Social job** — Look like a shop that runs properly: a written quote, a client portal with job status, a card payment that just works.

## Success Metrics

- **Call-to-lead capture:** share of inbound calls that produce a lead record with a transcript. This is the metric the call-routing feature exists for, and the one a shop can compare against its previous zero.
- **Quote-to-booking conversion:** share of issued quotes that become scheduled jobs, tracked per service type so the shop learns which work it actually wins.
- **Bay and tech utilization:** booked hours against available bay-hours per week. The listing's promise of tracking bays and techs is worth nothing unless the shop can see idle capacity.
- **Per-job margin visibility:** share of completed jobs with material and labour cost attached, so profitability analytics reflect real cost rather than a guess.
- **QuickBooks reconciliation lag:** days between payment and the synced entry appearing. The bookkeeper's adoption depends on this being near zero.
- **Follow-up recovery:** bookings that originate from an automated follow-up on an unanswered call — revenue that did not exist before the platform.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Field-service platforms (Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan)** — mature CRM, scheduling, invoicing and QuickBooks sync, built for trades that travel to a customer. They model a route and a technician, not a bay and a cure time, and they know nothing about film coverage or vehicle-specific quoting.
- **Automotive shop management systems (Tekmetric, Shop-Ware, Mitchell 1)** — built around mechanical repair orders, parts catalogues and labour guides. A wrap shop's unit of work is coverage area and material, which those catalogues do not carry.
- **Generic CRM plus calendar plus Square** — what most shops run today. Nothing connects the call to the quote to the bay, so every handoff is retyped and per-job margin is unknowable.
- **Instagram DMs and a phone number** — the real incumbent for smaller shops. Free, immediate, and the reason leads disappear when the owner is under a car.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] Confirm call-recording consent requirements per operating jurisdiction before enabling transcripts, since two-party-consent regions require announced recording.
- [ ] QuickBooks Online production sync requires app review; establish that timeline before promising accounting integration at launch.
- [ ] Validate AI quoting against real shop rates and vehicle coverage data. A confidently wrong quote costs the shop the job or the margin, and either one ends the trial.
- [ ] Decide whether inventory tracks film by roll, by linear metre or by job consumption — the choice determines whether material cost per job is real or estimated.
- [ ] The source is a maker-written product listing, not a customer complaint. No shop count, price point or adoption figure is stated, so the demand evidence here is weaker than it reads.
- [ ] Test one-handed phone use in a bay for job-stage updates before assuming techs will keep the job board current.
