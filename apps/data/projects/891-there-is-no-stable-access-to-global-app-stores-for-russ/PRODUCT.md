# There is no stable access to global app stores for Russian developers

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

When Google Play removes your app or Apple rejects it, stop guessing: get the likely policy behind the notice, the corrective steps that resolved comparable cases, and a documented path toward monetisation — instead of trial and error that costs 2–3 weeks of updates and revenue each time.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Russian dev team stuck on RuStore | Cannot launch globally; Apple monetisation blocked, Play removals every six months, 2–3 weeks lost each time. |
| Any developer facing opaque store enforcement | The author's core complaint is not the rules but the absence of stated reasons and any ready-made scheme. |
| Lawyers and incorporation advisers | The offshore-entity route raised in the comments is their work; a documented pipeline sends them qualified cases. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Understand why a specific rejection or removal happened and what has actually fixed it before.
2. **Emotional job** — Stop losing two to three weeks twice a year to a process nobody will explain.
3. **Social job** — Ship globally as a professional team, rather than being confined to one national store.

## Success Metrics

- **Time to restoration:** days from removal to a restored listing, against the author's current 2–3 weeks.
- **Diagnosis accuracy:** share of decoded notices where the identified policy matched the platform's eventual stated reason.
- **Case coverage:** number of documented, resolved cases in the knowledge base — the asset that makes the diagnosis better than a guess.
- **Monetisation outcomes:** teams that reach paid distribution after following the documented path, which is the author's stated priority.

## Pricing & Monetization

10,000 rubles (about $110) per year as an annual team subscription — the author's own figure. At that price the delivery has to be a knowledge product plus monitoring: per-case legal representation costs more than the annual fee in a single hour, so any legal work is a referral, not an inclusion.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Trial and error, the incumbent.** The team's current method, in the author's words, because there are no ready-made schemes; it costs huge amounts of time and resources.
- **Platform documentation and appeal forms** — the official channel, and the source of the transparency complaint: no clear warnings and no exact reasons.
- **Offshore incorporation via lawyers** — the route a commenter recommends, with non-Russian-passport UBOs as controlling shareholders and contract structures protecting the Russian UBOs. Described as a matter of time and moderate money; it addresses monetisation more directly than any software can, at a different price.
- **RuStore only** — the status quo the team is confined to, which is distribution without a global market.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] The author asks for a solution that guarantees resolution of suspensions. No third party can guarantee a platform's decision, and promising it would be the central dishonesty available here.
- [ ] The Apple monetisation block appears to be a jurisdiction-level restriction. If so, the only real remedy is corporate restructuring, which is legal work outside a $110/year subscription.
- [ ] Building a case knowledge base requires other teams to share their removals and resolutions; the source gives no indication such a community dataset exists.
- [ ] Advising on offshore structures for sanctioned-jurisdiction developers carries compliance exposure for the service itself, which the source does not consider.
