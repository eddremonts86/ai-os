# Research: existing solutions cover only 30% of small businesses' concerns about potential legal risks. They need a different product.

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

Small business owners with a website carry a worry they cannot name: that something on the site invites a fine. The tools they buy answer the part that is easy to package — privacy policy, cookie banner, terms — which the research puts at 20-30% of the actual concern. This product goes after the rest: scan the live site, name the risks in plain language, say which jurisdiction makes each one a risk, and separate what the owner can fix from what needs a lawyer.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Small online store owner | The furniture-store owner who started the research: worried his site holds risks that lead to fines, and the tools he could buy did not address most of it. |
| The 28 interviewed owners | Across niches and countries, all reported the same problem. The research states they are ready to use the future solution, which makes them the launch cohort rather than a market segment. |
| Freelancer or agency maintaining the site | Gets asked "are we compliant?" and currently has no way to answer beyond installing a cookie banner. |
| The research team | Offers the first three developers with a working MVP a personal presentation to the respondents plus their direct contacts — their interest is that someone builds it. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Find out what on my website could actually get me fined, in language I understand, without hiring counsel to look.
2. **Emotional job** — Replace a background worry with a finite list. The owner's problem was described as a background concern, not a known violation.
3. **Social job** — Be able to tell a partner, a marketplace or a payment provider that the site has been checked, and show what was checked.

## Success Metrics

- **Coverage against the stated gap:** share of a respondent's named concerns that the scan produces a finding for. The whole premise is that incumbents cover 20-30%; the number this product reaches is the only metric that tests it.
- **Finding acceptance:** share of findings an owner marks as relevant rather than noise. A scanner that produces fifty irrelevant risks recreates the anxiety it was meant to bound.
- **Remediation completion:** share of self-fixable findings actually resolved within 30 days, which separates a report from a product.
- **Respondent conversion:** how many of the 28 become active users when shown a working MVP. The research says they are willing; this is the reading of whether that survives contact with the real thing.
- **Re-scan retention:** share of accounts that run a second scheduled scan. A one-time audit is a purchase; a recurring scan is a business.

## Pricing & Monetization

The only price in the source is $99 for the research document itself, sold through the team's Telegram community and available free in exchange for five social posts. That is the researchers' price for the research, not a validated price for the product. The document is said to contain the monetization model the respondents prefer — which means the pricing decision is knowable but not currently known. Naming a subscription figure here would invent exactly the fact the research was conducted to establish.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Termly, iubenda, CookieYes** — named in the source. Focused on privacy, cookies and basic legal documentation, which the research assesses as covering at most 20-30% of what owners are worried about. They are not the competition for the remaining 70%; they are the evidence it is unserved.
- **The broader Legal AI market** — the source values it at $3.11 billion and growing, without naming which products in it address SMB website risk. That gap is the research's central claim.
- **A lawyer on retainer** — complete and jurisdiction-aware, priced beyond a small online store, and reactive rather than continuous.
- **Doing nothing** — the actual incumbent. The furniture-store owner's state before the conversation: aware of the risk, unable to size it, so carrying it.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] The research document, which contains the target-audience profile, the exact product wanted, the preferred monetization model, the competitive analysis and the go-to-market plan, costs $99 and is not in hand. Obtain it before scoping past the scan, or accept that the scope is a guess at contents that already exist.
- [ ] Validate the 20-30% coverage claim by mapping incumbent feature sets against the respondents' named concerns. It is a research conclusion, not a benchmark.
- [ ] Legal-risk findings without counsel review invite the liability the product is meant to reduce. Decide the disclaimer and review model before the first scan ships.
- [ ] Respondents span multiple countries; establish which jurisdictions the first version claims to cover and refuse findings outside them rather than guessing.
- [ ] The offer of respondent access applies to the first three MVPs. Confirm whether that window is still open before treating the 28 contacts as an asset.
- [ ] The research is sold by the party asserting the problem is real, and the guarantee offered is free access to another validated problem if it is not. That is an incentive worth pricing into how much of the scope rests on the document's claims.
