# Repoopulate – Follow step-by-step diet protocols to rebuild your gut after disruption

## Problem

Repoopulate guides people through what to eat to rebuild a healthy gut microbiome after a colonoscopy, antibiotics or food poisoning. The BetaList capture describes a structured recovery product: protocols organized into tracks and day-by-day phases, explanations of which foods to choose or avoid and why, practical meal suggestions paired with clear biochemical reasoning, and a focus on protecting the mucosal barrier, reducing irritation and restoring digestion during the first critical days of gut recovery. The goal is confident, gentle food choices in a short, step-driven protocol.

## Objective

Turn post-disruption gut recovery into a followable product: step-by-step diet protocols organized by track and day, with food guidance that explains the biochemical why, so a user coming out of a colonoscopy, antibiotics or food poisoning knows what to eat in the first critical days without guessing.

## Target Users

- Patients recovering from a colonoscopy who want guidance for the days after the procedure.
- People finishing an antibiotic course who want to rebuild their gut microbiome deliberately.
- Anyone recovering from food poisoning who needs gentle, low-irritation food choices.

## MVP Scope

- Recovery tracks for the three named disruptions: colonoscopy, antibiotics, food poisoning.
- Day-by-day phases inside each track.
- Per-phase food guidance: what to choose, what to avoid, and the reasoning for each.
- Meal suggestions paired with the biochemical explanation.
- A short, completable protocol flow (the site is described as a short protocol).

## Constraints

- The capture is a BetaList listing; no clinical-credentials or author-expertise claims appear in it.
- Health-adjacent content: the MVP must stay dietary guidance, not medical advice, and avoid unverified therapeutic claims.
- Content quality is the whole product; the biochemical reasoning must be defensible and sourced.
- The value is in the first critical days, so the protocol must be short and gentle by design.
