# Nigerians can't access local movies due to expensive subscriptions and piracy

> Product brief authored from the source title and category. The poster's text was not available (source.name: manual); sections below re-state the problem and infer only what the title and category support.

## Value Proposition

A Nigerian viewer can watch a Nollywood first run on their phone for NGN 1,500/month - less than a single data top-up - and the producer gets a per-view royalty on a ledger they can read, instead of a piracy site that pays them nothing.

## Target Users

- Nigerian adult viewers (18-45) in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Kano who already watch Nollywood.
- Nigerian diaspora in the UK, US, Canada and South Africa who want Nollywood at home.
- Nollywood producers who want distribution that pays a clear per-view royalty above what they get from pirate sites (which is zero).

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** - Watch Nollywood without breaking the monthly mobile-data budget.
2. **Emotional job** - Stop feeling guilty about piracy when the legitimate option is unaffordable.
3. **Social job** - Be able to recommend a friend to a paid Nollywood app, not a Telegram channel.

## Success Metrics

- **Activation:** first episode watched within 24 hours of install.
- **Retention:** >= 40% monthly active for 3 consecutive months.
- **Producer payout:** mean producer receives a per-view royalty that is at least the equivalent of a single Lagos cinema ticket over 6 months.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Pirate sites and Telegram channels** - what viewers use today; no revenue to producers.
- **Netflix / Amazon Prime** - global catalogue; the Nigerian slice is small at global prices.
- **IrokoTV / Africa Magic** - existing paid Nollywood services; pricing and catalogue positioning is what the new entrant differentiates against.
