# The problem of lacking effective rescue robots for disaster situations

> Product brief authored from the source title and category. The poster's text was not available (source.name: manual); sections below re-state the problem and infer only what the title and category support.

## Value Proposition

An Indian NDRF or SDRF commander can deploy a tracked robot into a compromised structure within 90 seconds and see thermal, visual and audio from inside on a chest-worn tablet, at a procurement cost that fits a battalion's annual capital budget.

## Target Users

- Indian NDRF battalions and state SDRF teams handling post-collapse, flood and industrial incidents.
- Industrial hazmat teams in refineries and chemical plants needing reconnaissance without a human entry.
- Fire services in tier-1 Indian cities whose commanders want a tracked scout before committing crew.

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** - Find a victim in a space too dangerous to enter by hand.
2. **Emotional job** - Stop asking 'what if we'd sent someone in' after a collapse.
3. **Social job** - Give the commander a video record to share with the control room and the family.

## Success Metrics

- **Pilot metric:** 3 NDRF battalions complete at least 5 mock-deployments each within 60 days of receipt.
- **Time:** median time from power-on to first video frame under 90 seconds.
- **Reliability:** 99% mission uptime in pilot deployments (no shutdowns attributable to platform, not operator error).

## Competitive Landscape

- **Boston Dynamics Spot / Stratasys** - capable but priced at 5-10x the target.
- **DRDO Daksh** - India's own EOD robot; purpose-built for bomb disposal, not reconnaissance.
- **DJI / smaller Chinese units** - cheaper but spare-part-dependent and not designed for stair climbing through debris.
