# I can't imagine me succeeding

## Problem
 I failed many times, now I'm building SaaS and I want it to be successful. But I have some sort of reflex or feeling that it won't succeed. I'm trying to get rid of that thinking but it doesn't seem to work. Is it happening to you? What do you think and do you have any advice? Pls help! submitted by /u/Delicious-Action-351 [link] [comments]

---

## Objective

This is a short emotional / mindset post, not a product spec. A founder who has failed many times and is now building a SaaS that he wants to succeed reports a persistent reflex / feeling that this one will fail too. He says he has tried to get rid of the thought and it has not worked, and asks the r/SaaS community whether this happens to them, what they think, and whether anyone has advice. No product, vertical, customer, or failure-history detail is named.

## Target Users

- Primary: r/SaaS readers who have lived through repeated founder failure and recognise the same "it won't work" reflex, especially those who have a practical answer (not just sympathy).
- Secondary: anyone in a serial-failure pattern who is weighing whether the next attempt is worth it and is reading the thread for company and advice.
- Tertiary: founders who have successfully shipped after a stretch of failures and can describe what broke the reflex for them, if anything did.

## MVP Scope

No product is described. The closest thing to a deliverable is the open ask the post puts to the community:

1. Does this "it won't work" reflex happen to you?
2. What do you think is causing it (history bias, selection effect, real signal about the project, something else)?
3. What do you actually do about it — what works, what doesn't, what is just talk?

If anything were to be built from this post, it would be a founder-coaching or accountability tool. That is out of scope here.

## Constraints

- No product, no tech, no pricing, no go-to-market. The post is a request for lived experience and advice.
- The author does not name the SaaS, the failures, or the count of attempts. Replies that assume a specific history (e.g., "after three tries") over-fit.
- "Trying to get rid of that thinking" is described but the methods tried are not named. Replies that recommend a specific cognitive technique without knowing what has already been tried may recommend something he has already done.
- The post is short and the framing is emotional. Replies that diagnose him (anxiety, depression, impostor syndrome) are out of scope unless they are explicitly invited.
