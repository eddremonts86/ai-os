# Online clothes shopping is a lottery. There's no accessible technology to see how an item will fit your body, especially in small stores. It's a pain for the buyer and a loss for the seller

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

Drops into any seller's existing storefront in under an hour and gives their buyers a fit signal that drops return rate by an order that recovers the subscription cost.

## Target Users

Small Indian apparel sellers (Instagram boutiques, Meesho resellers, Shopify-store operators) with 50-5,000 monthly orders, plus the Indian buyers who shop from them.

## Jobs To Be Done

When I see a garment I like, I want to see it on my own body in the right size so that I can decide whether to order without guessing. When I run a small store, I want a fit widget that I can install without a developer so that I stop losing money on returns.

## Success Metrics

Return-rate reduction reported by pilot sellers. Widget install-to-first-use conversion on seller sites. Buyer-side repeat-use rate within 30 days.

## Competitive Landscape

Zeekit (acquired by Walmart) and Vue.ai operate at enterprise scale only. Indian virtual try-on startups (e.g. Metail) have largely pivoted. Meesho and Myntra have tried-on features but only inside their own apps, not embeddable. Source does not name a specific competitor by name.

## Risks & Open Questions

Garment-on-body fit realism is bounded by photo angle and lighting; user disappointment with mediocre render is a real risk. Biometric-data regulations in India are evolving. Need at least 5 pilot sellers in different apparel verticals before claims about return reduction are credible.
