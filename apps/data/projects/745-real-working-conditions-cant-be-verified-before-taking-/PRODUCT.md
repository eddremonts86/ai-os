# Real working conditions can't be verified before taking a job — reviews lie. Need a service for direct contact with former employees. Willing to pay: £50–100 when I successfully get hired.

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

Before you accept an offer, talk to someone who verifiably worked there in the last 6–12 months and has agreed to answer questions. Pay only if you take the job. The alternative the author lived through was hundreds of reviews of which only a small fraction matched reality, and unanswered LinkedIn messages.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Candidate with an offer in hand | Already lost several months and ended in burnout by trusting the interview description and the review sites. |
| Recent former employee | Knows what the job is actually like; has no reason to answer a stranger's cold message today. |
| Niche professional communities (photography Discords, Telegram groups) | Already hold these conversations, but with huge message volume and poor search — the author only thought to look after quitting. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Find out what the work is actually like before accepting, from someone who was recently inside.
2. **Emotional job** — Not spend another month training for a job that turns out to be something else.
3. **Social job** — Ask the awkward questions privately, without signalling distrust to the hiring company.

## Success Metrics

- **Reply rate:** share of matched former employees who actually answer — the metric that separates this from the author's failed LinkedIn attempts.
- **Time to first answer:** hours from request to a real reply, since the candidate is deciding on an offer deadline.
- **Recency compliance:** share of respondents whose employment ended within the 6–12 month window.
- **Outcome conversion:** share of conversations that end in a hire, since the £50–100 fee only lands then.

## Pricing & Monetization

£50–100 paid by the candidate when they successfully get hired — the author's own stated terms. Contingent pricing means nothing is charged for a conversation that talks them out of a bad job, which is arguably the more valuable outcome and is not monetised in this model.

## Competitive Landscape

The author names what they tried:

- **Glassdoor, Indeed and similar review sites** — hundreds of reviews, of which only a small fraction matched their real experience; some obviously fake positives, some likely-fake negatives, some contradictory and emotional.
- **Forums and Reddit** — searched, almost nothing found.
- **ChatGPT** — tried, and it cannot know what a specific employer's day-to-day is like.
- **Discord and Telegram groups for photographers** — the discussions do exist there, but with a huge flow of messages and difficult search.
- **Direct LinkedIn outreach** — messaged several employees, received no replies at all.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] Supply-side incentive is unsolved. The author's cold outreach got zero replies; the source does not say what would make a former employee answer, and the £50–100 only exists after a hire.
- [ ] Verifying that someone worked somewhere in the last 6–12 months, without asking for documents they will not hand over.
- [ ] Legal exposure: a respondent describing unpaid work processes at a named employer may be breaching an NDA or exposed to defamation claims.
- [ ] Contingent revenue means the service is unpaid for every conversation that correctly warns a candidate off. That inverts the incentive and needs rethinking before launch.
