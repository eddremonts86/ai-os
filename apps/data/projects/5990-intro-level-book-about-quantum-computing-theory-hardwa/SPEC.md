# SPEC.md — Intro-level book about quantum computing theory/hardware/applications

## Problem

Hi HN - If you&#x27;re anything like me, you&#x27;ve seen the occasional quantum computing posts here and skimmed through them, hoping that the comments would explain the significance (or lack thereof) of the results in question. I got to the point where I just wanted to read a proper book about the field, so that I could understand what quantum computing &#x27;is&#x27; at a high level (I&#x27;m technical, with an EE PhD, but not a physics or CS guy).<p>Unfortunately, I found that there wasn&#x27;t really a text that fit my needs. There are books that explain the math&#x2F;CS theory at an appropriate level (I recommend Scott Aaronson&#x27;s Quantum Computing Since Democritus and Thomas Wong&#x27;s Introduction to Classical and Quantum Computing), but nothing that deals with the hardware or the applications (both of which I am very interested in).<p>So I did a lot of self-education and paper-reading, and wrote the book I wanted to read. It&#x27;s called Quantum Computing for the Confused, and it&#x27;s available online (and in pdf&#x2F;epub) completely free. It&#x27;s fairly short (40k words) and meant to be accessible to audiences with a bit of technical literacy. I&#x27;ll also note that it is 100% human generated! :)<p>The book has four sections, each looking at a different aspect of quantum computing: its theory; how these systems are implemented in real hardware; what quantum computers may be used for; and what the industry looks like today. I think this work fills an important niche in layman-oriented writing about the field, which I think does not do a good job making itself intelligible to outsiders.<p>If you&#x27;re interested, you can find it here: <a href="https:&#x2F;&#x2F;quantumcomputingfortheconfused.com&#x2F;" rel="nofollow">https:&#x2F;&#x2F;quantumcomputingfortheconfused.com&#x2F;</a><p>Happy to discuss and chat further. Cheers!
