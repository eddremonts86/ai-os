# Atlas:Face – the study kit and notetaking app you'll never leave

## Value Proposition

Note-taking and studying are the same loop split across apps: capture, then review, then remember. Atlas:Face's promise is to reunite them — the study kit and the notes in one place, so the note you wrote becomes the flashcard you review on schedule, and the app earns its retention claim by being the system you do not have to leave. The MVP's job is that loop, working end to end.

**One-liner:** A study kit and notetaking app built around the review loop.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Students | Notes and exam prep in one tool instead of three subscriptions. |
| Lifelong learners | Capture without review is forgetting with extra steps. |
| Certification candidates | A review schedule that works around a day job. |
| Researchers | Connected notes that stay findable years later. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. Take a note quickly, without fighting the editor.
2. Turn notes into flashcards without re-typing everything.
3. Follow a review schedule that spaces cards correctly.
4. Find an old note by keyword or by connection.

## Success Metrics

- Notes created per active user per week.
- Share of notes converted into flashcards, the bridge metric between the two halves.
- Review streaks: consecutive days with a completed queue.
- Monthly retention, the metric the title's claim actually stands on.

- [ ] The retention claim is bold and unproven in the capture; churn would disprove it loudly.
- [ ] Both halves of the category are crowded; the fusion must be visibly better, not just combined.
- [ ] Note-to-flashcard conversion quality decides whether the bridge gets used.
- [ ] Switching costs from existing note apps are high; import must work.
- [ ] No monetization is stated; study tools face strong free alternatives.
