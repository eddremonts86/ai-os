# First time founders who are launching a startup right now: How are you leveraging AI? I will not promote

## Problem
 I've launched several SaaS products over the past couple decades. I'm a bit older now, and admittedly old-school in many ways. I'm curious how the new younger generation of first time founders actually build their startups now. The generation that has no bias on how things "should" be done; that are taking AI at face value and running with it. I ask because: • Non technical founders can now build prototype SaaS products w/o any engineering experience. • You can build faster than before. • Applying RAG to say Claude adds the potential of incredible automation, of so many things that people used to have to do. • Much of the marketing collateral that used to take time to build is now pretty easy to do. • it's now easy to have call transcripts, which give you lossless meeting notes. Valuable internally, and with customer calls to not miss a thing. They can also be fed into automated systems submitted by /u/zumpoof [link] [comments]

---

## Objective

This is a discussion / survey post, not a product spec. A serial SaaS founder (decades of experience) is asking the r/startups community — specifically first-time founders launching right now — how they are actually using AI across the build, marketing, and customer-call loops. The post lists five concrete areas where the author assumes AI is changing the work (prototyping, RAG automation, marketing collateral, lossless meeting notes, and transcript-driven automations) and asks for confirmation, contradiction, or extension. No product is proposed.

## Target Users

- Primary: r/startups readers who are first-time founders launching a startup right now and have integrated AI tools into their day-to-day build/marketing/ops loops.
- Secondary: older or "old-school" founders (the author's own cohort) who are curious whether the generational gap is as real as they suspect.
- Tertiary: anyone reading the thread who wants a snapshot of which AI tasks founders are treating as free wins in 2026 (prototype generation, RAG over Claude, marketing collateral, meeting notes, transcript pipelines).

## MVP Scope

No product is described. The closest thing to a deliverable is the five prompts the author lists, each of which is implicitly an open question:

1. Are non-technical founders actually shipping prototype SaaS without engineering help? How much of the prototype survives contact with real users?
2. Does RAG over Claude (or similar) unlock automation that used to require a person? Where has it actually paid off, and where has it been a waste?
3. Has marketing collateral (deck, site copy, screenshots, video) become meaningfully cheaper to produce for founders?
4. Are lossless meeting notes — from customer calls in particular — being used to drive product decisions in a way that wasn't possible before?
5. Are call transcripts being fed into downstream automation (CRM, support, follow-ups), and what does that pipeline look like?

The post is a request for lived examples in each of those five buckets.

## Constraints

- No product, no tech, no pricing, no go-to-market. The post is a "how are you doing it" question, not a buildable brief.

- The post does not ask for "the best AI tool" or compare named products — naming specific winners would be invention.
