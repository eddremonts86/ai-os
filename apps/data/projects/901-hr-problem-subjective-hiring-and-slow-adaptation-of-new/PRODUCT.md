# HR problem: subjective hiring and slow adaptation of new employees

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

Hire on evidence a novice recruiter can read the same way a senior one does, then onboard through practical tasks instead of text and video courses — priced per hire at 5,000–10,000 RUB against a mis-hire that costs 5,000–30,000 RUB in posting plus one to six months of slow adaptation.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| HR department with continuous openings | The problem is daily and systemic, and accompanies every new job opening. |
| Novice recruiter | Named in the source as least able to assess candidate skills objectively; carries the most decision risk. |
| Hiring manager | Pays for the mis-hire in posting costs and in one to six months of a new hire reaching productivity. |
| New employee | Currently onboarded through boring text and video courses with no practical skill development. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Decide between candidates on comparable evidence rather than the interviewer's impression.
2. **Emotional job** — Stop carrying the risk of a mis-hire on personal judgement, especially as a newer recruiter.
3. **Social job** — Run a hiring process the business trusts, and have new hires productive in weeks rather than months.

## Success Metrics

- **Time to productivity:** weeks from start date to full productivity, against the 1–6 month adaptation window she reports.
- **Assessment agreement:** whether a novice and a senior recruiter reach the same conclusion from the same assessment evidence — the direct test of the subjectivity claim.
- **Mis-hire rate:** early departures and failed probations per cohort, the cost the pricing is anchored to.
- **Screening depth:** share of candidates whose resumes were analysed beyond the ATS filter, which she says does not happen today.

## Pricing & Monetization

5,000–10,000 rubles ($55–110) per hired employee — her stated figure, explicitly derived from what a bad hire costs: 5,000–30,000 RUB in job posting alone, plus losses from an adaptation of one to six months. Per-hire pricing aligns the product with the outcome, and also means the service earns nothing from a search that correctly rejects everyone.

## Competitive Landscape

Drawn from what she says they use now:

- **Standard ATS** — used for initial screening by gender, age and experience. Fast, shallow, and the age and gender criteria are not defensible.
- **Recruiter experience** — the real decision mechanism today, and the source of the subjectivity she is trying to remove.
- **Numerous manual interviews** — thorough and the reason selection consumes enormous resources.
- **Text and video onboarding courses** — the current adaptation scheme, which she says lacks practical skill development.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] The current screening filters by gender and age. Any product touching this pipeline has to refuse those criteria rather than automate them, which is a change to her existing process and needs saying out loud.
- [ ] Assessment tasks that predict on-the-job performance are role-specific. The source names no role or industry, so it is unknown how many task libraries would be needed.
- [ ] "Objectivity" is the stated goal but she already notes no method gives 100% of it. Selling objectivity would be an overclaim; comparability is the deliverable.
- [ ] Per-hire pricing means revenue only on successful hires, while assessment cost is incurred for every candidate — a volume-dependent model with no data in the source on hiring volume.
