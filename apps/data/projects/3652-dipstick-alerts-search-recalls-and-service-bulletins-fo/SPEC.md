# Dipstick Alerts – Search recalls and service bulletins for your car

## Problem

The capture is unusually complete and is quoted directly from the author. He bought a 2019 Subaru Forester with an extended warranty and started checking NHTSA.gov monthly for new service bulletins. A bulletin about a trunk not opening as far in cold weather pointed him to a problem he would not otherwise have recognised as repairable, and he got it fixed under warranty. He wanted a simpler way to find information relevant to his car and to be notified when something new appeared, so he built Dipstick Alerts as his first substantial project with extensive AI-coding-tool help.

The site's behaviour is stated explicitly. A visitor can use it without signing up. They select a year, make and model and search for recalls and manufacturer service bulletins. Each result carries a plain-language summary, symptom notes, suggested next steps, and a link to the original manufacturer document when one is available. Email alerts are optional.

The data and the pipeline are also stated. NHTSA is the underlying source, so coverage is currently US-focused; some information may still be useful elsewhere but applicability can differ between markets. A daily process scans for newly published recalls and bulletins, imports them through queues, matches them to applicable vehicles, tracks superseding bulletins, and prepares alerts. Some of the plain-language content is generated from the source documents using Gemini, but the original manufacturer documents remain the authoritative source. The application runs on Cloudflare Workers using D1, Queues, KV and email sending.

The author also names what he wants feedback on: incorrect vehicle matches, whether the summaries are genuinely useful, anything confusing in the search or alert flow, and how the site performs under load.

## Objective

Ship a public, no-signup-needed site, named Dipstick Alerts, that lets a visitor select a year, make and model and see the recalls and manufacturer service bulletins that apply to their vehicle, with a plain-language summary on each result and an optional email alert when something new is published. The data pipeline is a daily scan of NHTSA, normalised through Cloudflare Queues into D1, with plain-language content generated by Gemini and the manufacturer documents remaining authoritative.

## Target Users

- Owners of US-sold vehicles who bought an extended warranty and want to know what is covered without checking NHTSA.gov every month.
- Owners who suspect a behaviour their car has (a trunk that does not open as far, a transmission that hesitates in cold weather) and want to find out whether it is a known issue.
- Buyers considering a specific year, make and model who want to see the recall and bulletin history before purchase.
- Mechanics and service advisors who want a quick way to look up the bulletin history of a customer's vehicle.
- Anyone who has been burned by a recall they did not know about and wants a passive alert stream.

## MVP Scope

- A public, no-signup search page where the visitor selects year, make and model and sees the recalls and manufacturer service bulletins that apply.
- A per-result view carrying a plain-language summary, symptom notes, suggested next steps and a link to the original manufacturer document when available.
- Optional email alerts: a visitor can subscribe with an email and a vehicle selection, and the system delivers alerts when new recalls or bulletins match.
- A daily pipeline that scans NHTSA for newly published recalls and bulletins, normalises them and writes them to D1 through Queues.
- A vehicle-match step that maps a bulletin or recall to the applicable year, make and model combinations, with the match logic auditable.
- A superseding-bulletin tracker that links a bulletin to its successor so a visitor does not act on a stale entry.
- A plain-language generation step that calls Gemini over the source document and writes the summary, with the original manufacturer document kept as the authoritative link.
- A status page that shows when the last daily scan ran, how many new entries were imported and the current queue depth.

## Constraints

- NHTSA is the source of truth, so coverage is US-focused and the site must say so on every page where coverage matters.
- The original manufacturer documents are authoritative; Gemini-generated plain-language content is supplementary and must always link back to the source.
- The site must work without signup. Email alerts are optional, not a precondition for searching.
- The pipeline is daily, so the freshness budget is 24 hours, and a missed daily run is a visible failure on the status page.
- The vehicle-match logic is the author-named risk, so it must be auditable and the author is asking for reports of incorrect matches.
- The tech stack is fixed in the post (Cloudflare Workers, D1, Queues, KV, email sending) and is the right shape for this workload.
