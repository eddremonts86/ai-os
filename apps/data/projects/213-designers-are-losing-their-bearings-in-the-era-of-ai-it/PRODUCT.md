# Designers are losing their bearings in the era of AI — it's unclear which direction to develop in, who reviews work, and how to keep the craft.

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

Get a real direction in the AI era without burning out on tutorials. The first 500 paid mentorships self-fund the curriculum team from the recovered focus of mid-career designers.

## Target Users

Mid-career designers in Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, and Central Asia who feel professionally unmoored by AI. Also junior designers trying to break in and looking for a structured alternative to the showcase sites.

## Jobs To Be Done

Functional: have a clear plan and a trusted reviewer. Emotional: stop the imposter feeling of always being behind. Social: be the designer who has a visible direction, not just a feed of pretty shots.

## Success Metrics

At least 50% of paying users complete their first 3-month track. Mentor review rating above 4.5 out of 5. Curriculum completion rate above 30% across active users.

## Competitive Landscape

Dribbble and Behance are showcases, not learning loops. Design Twitter is a feed, not a curriculum. Generic mentorship platforms (ADPList, MentorCruise) are mentor directories without a curriculum. No mainstream service combines a curated curriculum with a vetted mentor pool for designers in the post-Soviet market.

## Risks & Open Questions

Mentor quality can drop if the vetting loosens. Designers may resist the structure and prefer the open feed. Curriculum updates must keep pace with AI tool changes. Reviews may slip into shallow feedback if quality control fails.
