## Value Proposition

_Based on source brief:_ In data science and analytics, it is often somewhat difficult for new analysts to get a grasp of the field before actually starting at a real job. During education, the task is often contained and clean: &quot;Here&#x27;s churn.csv, choose a model and report the metrics.&quot; But on the job it is often much less clear, much more expansive, and beckons a much more holistic judgment: &quot;The Director is meeting the Legislature at noon Monday, this needs to be on my desk by 4 on Friday. Here&#x27;s a handful of tables that might be helpful, but there are many idiosyncrasies in them. Also, we aren&#x27;t entirely sure what about the Director&#x27;s meeting is supported by the data, but I&#x27;m sure you can figure it out. Good luck.&quot; I built The Analyst as a flight simulator of sorts for the field. You get dropped in as a new analyst in a fictional company with millions of rows of data and dozens of tables and asked to develop real products exercising real judgment. It&#x27;s all free and open-source and requires no accounts, signups, or personal info, and all of it exists in the browser. You perform SQL, Python, and the summary work inside the browser, and there&#x27;s export options for GitHub portfolios, to share socially, or to give to your instructor for evaluation. And yes, there&#x27;s an Instructor Desk for those teachers, giving them guidance and direction. Importantly, there are no answer keys anywhere: the website gives concepts and ideas you should pick up on, but the deep judgment evaluation is left for another human. I&#x27;m definitely excited about it, and while there are a lot of places online that encompass parts of the vision here, almost all require payment and are closed-source. I truly hope this site proves useful for many people! I&#x27;d love any feedback you all have. Thanks much.
