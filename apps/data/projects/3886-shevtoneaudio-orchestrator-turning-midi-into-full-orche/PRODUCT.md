# ShevtoneAudio Orchestrator – Turning MIDI into Full Orchestration

## Value Proposition

AI as an orchestration tool, not a replacement for the musician. Orchestrator takes the composer's own MIDI, analyzes harmony, melody, rhythm, dynamics, structure and orchestral density, and develops a full arrangement across strings, brass, percussion and other sections — then hands back editable MIDI, so the composer keeps control and continues working inside their DAW. Two years of development by a composer-developer, at a limited launch price of $149.

**One-liner:** Turn your own MIDI into a full orchestration you can still edit — strings, brass, percussion, back to your DAW.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| MIDI-writing composers | Their own material is developed into a full orchestration instead of being replaced by generated music. |
| DAW-centric producers | Everything comes back as editable MIDI they can keep working with. |
| Music-tech evaluators | A concrete claim to test: AI as orchestration assistant rather than composer replacement. |

The author explicitly asks for feedback from developers, composers and music-technology people; that defines the audiences.

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Feed a composer's own MIDI into the tool and get a developed orchestration back.

2. **Functional job** — Analyze harmony, melody, rhythm, dynamics, structure and orchestral density of the material.

3. **Functional job** — Arrange across strings, brass, percussion and other sections.

4. **Emotional job** — Keep authorship: the composer stays in control, editing everything inside the DAW.

## Success Metrics

- **MIDI fidelity:** the output remains editable MIDI that opens and keeps working in the composer's DAW.
- **Section coverage:** arrangements span strings, brass, percussion and other sections as stated.
- **Musical analysis:** harmony, melody, rhythm, dynamics, structure and orchestral density are all analyzed.
- **Paid adoption:** composers buy at the $149 launch offer and produce real pieces with it.

## Pricing & Monetization

The author states a limited launch offer for Orchestrator at $149. No other pricing tiers or the regular price are stated.

## Competitive Landscape

The post names no competitors. The category is AI-assisted composition — generative music models, MIDI tools and orchestration software. The stated differentiator is philosophical and practical at once: generative tools produce finished pieces and sideline the composer, while Orchestrator works from the composer's own MIDI and returns editable MIDI, positioning AI as an orchestration tool.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] Two years of solo development with no cited users; the $149 launch is the first market test described.
- [ ] Editable MIDI quality across a full orchestral arrangement is hard; bad voice-leading or clashing density would be audible immediately.
- [ ] The demo is a site visit, not a downloadable trial; evaluation friction is unstated.
- [ ] The breakthrough claim (AI as orchestration tool) rests on composers accepting MIDI-level workflow rather than audio-grade generation.
