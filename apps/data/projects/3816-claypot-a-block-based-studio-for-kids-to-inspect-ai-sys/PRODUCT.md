# Claypot – a block-based studio for kids to inspect AI systems

## Value Proposition

AI literacy at block height. Claypot borrows the block-based programming metaphor kids already know from tools like Scratch and points it at the systems now surrounding them: instead of just using an AI, a child assembles blocks that poke at one — send this input, change that setting, compare the two answers — and watches the machine's behavior change in response. The educational bet is that inspection, not consumption, is the durable skill of an AI-native generation, and that the right instrument is a studio of small, visible, manipulable pieces rather than a lecture or a magic black box.

**One-liner:** A block-based studio where kids assemble experiments to inspect how AI systems behave.

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Snap together blocks that drive an AI interaction (prompt, input, setting).
2. **Functional job** — Run the experiment and watch the AI's response change as inputs change.
3. **Functional job** — See an explanation of what happened, in child-readable terms.
4. **Emotional job** — Feel like the AI is something you can examine and understand, not something that happens to you.

## Success Metrics

- **Experiment completion:** share of kids who assemble and run a full block experiment in a session.
- **Repeat experimentation:** experiments run per child per week — the habit of inspection.
- **Classroom adoption:** teachers or parents returning with the same child cohort over multiple sessions.
- **Explanation quality:** comprehension check scores on what the experiment showed (if built into the studio).
