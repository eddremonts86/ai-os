# Claypot – a block-based studio for kids to inspect AI systems

## Problem

The capture is a URL-only Show HN by arvindm1991 pointing at claypot.app; the post carries no description beyond the title, and the linked site did not render content during verification, so the product claim is the title itself. What the title states: Claypot is a block-based studio — the familiar drag-and-drop programming metaphor — whose purpose is letting kids inspect AI systems. Read literally, the promise is that a child can snap together blocks to look inside how an AI behaves: what it answers, what it sees, what changes when inputs change — an educational instrument for the generation growing up with these systems rather than a toy that hides the machinery. The capture offers no feature list, no curriculum, no pricing and no author context beyond the handle, so every structural claim beyond the title is inference from the stated category.

## Objective

Make AI systems inspectable by children: a block-based environment where a kid assembles small experiments — prompts, inputs, tweaks — and watches the AI's behavior change, with every mechanism visible and explainable.

## Target Users

- Kids (the stated audience) who encounter AI daily and deserve tools to understand it.
- Parents and educators who want a structured, safe way to introduce AI concepts.
- Curriculum designers looking for block-based activities around AI behavior.

## MVP Scope

- A block-based editor (drag, snap, run) as the interaction model.
- One or more inspectable AI behaviors: prompt-and-response, input tweaking, output comparison.
- A visual readout of what the system did and what changed.
- Child-safe defaults: no account, no personal data collection, no open-ended web access.
- A hosted studio at claypot.app.

- Audience safety: a product for kids inspecting AI needs content and interaction guardrails by default.
