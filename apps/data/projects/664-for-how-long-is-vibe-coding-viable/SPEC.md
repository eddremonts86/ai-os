# For how long is vibe coding viable?

## Problem
 AI has enabled creation of many vibe coded software. But while it may be easy to release a simple app, a vibe-coder, especially someone with no software dev experience will struggle with a full fledged app. Now that AI coding has been around for some time, is the impact to SaaS really overstated? Given the rising costs of AI, how long can a vibe-coder be able to develop and release software? Also when he finds that the app that he releases does not have traction, won't he just give up, stop vibe coding and exit the SaaS scene? How much money can a vibe-coder burn anyway, before he realizes that it is not possible to make money? So I feel that given a bit more time, things will stabilize, in terms of the SaaS market. And the ones left in the scene, will be the real software developers, with the needed experience to release proper software. What are your thoughts? submitted by /u/gittrics [link] [comments]

---

## Objective

The author is offering a prediction about the SaaS market: "vibe-coded" software built by non-developers using AI tools will be transient, because (a) full-fledged apps are still hard to ship without software-dev experience, (b) the rising cost of AI bounds how much a vibe-coder can afford to spend before giving up, and (c) the apps that don't get traction will be abandoned. The author predicts the market will stabilise and the survivors will be real software developers. The post is a thesis, not a product.

## Target Users

- Primary: r/SaaS readers — founders, developers, and observers of the AI-coding boom — to whom the author is pitching a stabilisation thesis.
- Secondary: vibe-coders who will read this as either a warning or a challenge.
- Tertiary: experienced SaaS developers, who the author predicts will be the eventual survivors.

## MVP Scope

No product. The post is a market-thesis opinion. The closest thing to a deliverable is the three-part argument the author makes:

1. A vibe-coder without software-dev experience will struggle with a full-fledged app.
2. Rising AI costs cap how much a vibe-coder can burn before giving up.
3. Apps without traction will be abandoned; the survivors will be the ones with the engineering experience to ship proper software.
