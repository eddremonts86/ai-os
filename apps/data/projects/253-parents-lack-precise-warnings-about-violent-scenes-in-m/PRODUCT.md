# Parents lack precise warnings about violent scenes in movies to safely watch films with their children

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

A database of timestamped, severity-tagged content warnings for films popular with Indian families — what is in the film, where in the film it happens, and how severe it is. The parent checks the page before pressing play, decides whether to watch with the child, and if they decide yes, uses the timestamps to skip the scenes they would rather their child not see.

## Target Users

- Indian parents deciding whether a film is appropriate for a specific child.
- Indian parents running a movie night with mixed-age children and needing to know which scenes to skip.
- Schools, libraries, and PTAs in India organising children's film screenings and needing an evidence-backed decision.
- Streaming platforms in India wanting a third-party content-warning layer alongside their own age ratings.

## Jobs To Be Done

- When my child asks to watch a new film tonight, I want to look it up and see a list of violent scenes with timestamps, so I can decide before pressing play.
- When I have decided to watch the film with my 9-year-old, I want a list of timestamps I can use to skip the worst scenes, so I do not have to pause and judge in real time.
- When I am a teacher organising a school screening, I want an evidence-backed per-scene severity list, so I can defend the choice to parents.
- When I am a streaming platform, I want a third-party warning layer I can show alongside my own age rating, so my users can make a finer-grained decision.

## Success Metrics

- Number of films in the curated catalogue at month six (coverage is the product's main constraint).
- Number of parent visits per week per film in the catalogue (proxy for usefulness, since the decision is per-film-per-child).
- Severity distribution: percentage of films with at least one "severe" warning (sanity check; if zero, the curation is too lenient).
- Number of curators contributing per month (proxy for sustainable coverage growth).

## Risks & Open Questions

- Validate problem with 5 Indian parent interviews before MVP: confirm that the precise-warning framing matches what parents actually want, and that they would check a third-party site before pressing play.
- Spoiler discipline: curators must describe scenes without giving away plot. A wrong culture here destroys trust; the MVP must enforce a "no plot, no outcome, no character name" rule in the warning text.
- Coverage growth is the binding constraint. A catalogue of 50 films is a demo; 5,000 is a product. The MVP must size the curator workforce against the coverage target before promising scope.
- Cultural-fit curation: warnings must reflect what Indian parents gate on, not just translate a Western rubric. The MVP needs at least one curator who can speak to the local context for each language it covers.
- Severity disagreement: two curators may disagree. The MVP must surface the curator's name and review date, and must allow multiple curators per film rather than pretending severity is mechanical.
