# SOC 2 fast-track for pre-Series A B2B SaaS — manual-first over tooling-first

## Problem

Something I keep seeing with early B2B SaaS teams (and lived myself): you never wake up wanting SOC 2. You wake up because an enterprise prospect won't sign until they see a report, and suddenly a thing you never thought about is sitting between you and revenue you already counted on. A few things I wish someone had told me sooner: The trigger is external, so the clock is external too. You don't want a GRC career, you want the deal signed. Optimize for time-to-audit, not for building the perfect program. The tooling is maybe 30% of the work. Roughly 40-60% of controls are human processes: someone has to own the quarterly access review, someone has to actually log in. Six months after buying a platform, the classic failure is a half-populated dashboard and three control owners who never came back. Buying expensive software doesn't assign ownership. Picking wrong is expensive in a way that hurts a small team. It's one of the fastest ways to burn $15k-$30k on a platform that added work instead of removing it. And the tools that look cheapest in year one can carry renewal surprises by year three, so ask the eighteen-month question before you sign, not after. For a 2-5 engineer pre-Series A shop, honestly manual with good templates is sometimes faster than fighting an automation dashboard you don't have headcount to feed. The real blocker for most people isn't the audit, it's 'where do I even start.' Turning that jargon into a plain checklist you can work through is what flips you from overwhelmed to in control. Curious how others handled their first one, did you go tooling-first or manual-first, and would you make the same call again? submitted by /u/ChalupaBrock [link] [comments]

## Objective

Get a pre-Series A B2B SaaS team from 'blocked deal' to 'audit-ready' on SOC 2 in the shortest possible time — using a manual-first playbook with templates, not the typical automation-first GRC tooling purchase.

## Target Users

2–5 engineer pre-Series A B2B SaaS teams whose first enterprise deal is blocked by a SOC 2 requirement. The author cites this exact profile and warns that tooling-first often burns $15k–$30k without delivering the deal.

## MVP Scope

A plain-checklist playbook + ownership-mapping template + 4–6 week timeline. Service shape: a 6-week engagement that takes the founder from 'where do I start' to audit-ready, with the founder owning the controls (no half-populated dashboard).

## Constraints
