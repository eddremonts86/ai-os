# Designers are losing their bearings in the era of AI — it's unclear which direction to develop in, which stack to master, and how to become a «highly valuable specialist».

## Problem

The author is a UX/UI designer working on product design for B2B and B2C SaaS in CRM, Sales Automation, HR, TravelTech, FinTech and MedTech. They say the market is changing so fast they no longer understand which way to move: big tech is hiring people with narrow, deep expertise, traditional hiring is dying, and to stay in demand you either become a uniquely strong specialist or assemble what they call a "terminator made of agents" — a system that lets one person cover the output of an entire studio. Three specific things are missing, in their words: there is no "stack for an AI interaction architect", no simulator where they can calibrate their skills (visuals, working with AI, the ability to simplify complex scenarios), and no feedback that would tell them whether their chosen direction will hit the mark. They are torn between options, time is running out, and the fear of choosing wrong is paralysing. The problem recurs every time they think about their development, see another AI news story, or talk to colleagues from Yandex or T-Bank. They have tried vibe coding, following new LLMs, professional chats and reading how people in big tech work: a sea of information, scattered, with no system. Courses do not help — they lag, because by the time a course is written new models and approaches have shipped, and courses teach you to "push buttons" when what they need is to design whole systems of agents. The unanswered question they state plainly: "What exactly should I learn to be in that tiny minority that will survive and make money in a year?" They are currently looking for work and say they are ready to participate in building a startup around this, because the cost of a wrong choice is losing their profession.

## Objective

Give a working designer a specific development trajectory instead of a sea of scattered advice: teach them to design and orchestrate a system of agents, let them calibrate their skills against a rubric in a simulator, and give feedback on whether their chosen direction holds — the four things the author names as what a solution must do.

## Target Users

- Primary: mid-to-senior product designers whose market is repricing under AI, who need to choose a direction now and are paralysed by the cost of choosing wrong. The author's own profile: UX/UI, B2B and B2C SaaS across CRM, Sales Automation, HR, TravelTech, FinTech and MedTech.
- Secondary: designers looking for work who need a demonstrable, current skill set rather than a course certificate — the author is job-hunting while asking this question.

## MVP Scope

The four capabilities the author lists as the definition of an effective tool:

- Trajectory: determine a development direction for this specific designer in the new market, rather than a generic curriculum.
- Agent-system design: teach how to design and orchestrate a system of agents — the "terminator made of agents" that covers a studio's output single-handed.
- Simulator: practise and calibrate skills on real scenarios, with visuals, working with AI, and simplifying complex scenarios as the axes they name.
- Feedback: a signal on whether the chosen direction is likely to hit the mark, which is what neither courses nor professional chats give them.

## Constraints

- Content staleness is the disqualifier. The author's stated reason courses fail is that new models and approaches ship before a course finishes being written. Anything with a fixed curriculum recreates the failure.
- It must teach system design, not tool operation. They explicitly reject being taught to "push buttons".
- No price is named. The author says they are ready to discuss payment and monetisation options with developers, and notes they are currently job-hunting and open to co-founding — so willingness to pay is real but unquantified.
- The differentiating claim is being in the "top 1%" rather than copying everyone else, which means a graded, personalised output — not the same trajectory handed to every user.
