# Aviation safety expert seeks a technical co-founder to develop an AI solution that prevents daily collisions between aircraft and birds/drones — a problem causing massive losses.

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

A fixed-site AI detection system that ingests radar tracks and optical frames at a busy airport, classifies incoming contacts (bird flock, single bird, drone), and emits a collision-risk alert to the tower before the contact reaches an active aircraft. The system runs in shadow mode first, building a measured record of recall and false-alarm rate, so the operational case for adoption is evidence rather than promise.

## Target Users

- Airport operations directors responsible for wildlife-strike prevention and runway safety.
- Tower supervisors and air-traffic controllers who need a clear, low-false-alarm early warning.
- Civil aviation authorities (DGAC France, EASA) that set the standards a detection system must meet.
- Drone-detection buyers: airport security, critical-infrastructure operators, large event organisers.

## Jobs To Be Done

- When a flock of birds is rising near the approach corridor, I want a collision-risk alert 30–60 seconds before closest approach, so the tower can act before a strike.
- When an unauthorised drone appears near the runway, I want detection and classification within seconds, so security can engage the appropriate protocol.
- When I am the aviation-safety expert, I want a technical co-founder who can build and ship the perception stack, so the system can move from idea to pilot.
- When I am an airport director evaluating a detection vendor, I want a measured recall and false-alarm rate from a real pilot, so I can compare against the cost of the strikes we already absorb.

## Success Metrics

- Recall on true positive contacts (bird or drone on collision course) during the shadow season.
- False-alarm rate per shift (a hard operational metric; the MVP must publish it).
- Mean time-to-alert from contact acquisition to tower warning.
- Number of contacts classified correctly during the validation season (proxy for perception-pipeline quality).

## Risks & Open Questions

- Co-founder fit: the post is explicit that the expert is looking for a technical partner. Compatibility on vision, IP, and risk tolerance is a precondition, not a deliverable.
- Certification pathway: an operational aviation-safety product requires type approval (EASA / DGAC). The MVP is shadow-mode advisory, so certification is not blocking the pilot, but the post-pilot roadmap must include it.
- Sensor placement cooperation: the airport must host the radar and cameras. Without that cooperation the pilot cannot run.
- False-alarm economics: a noisy alert is worse than no alert because the tower mutes it. The MVP must measure and report the false-alarm rate honestly and tune for it before declaring success.
- Weather and lighting envelope: optical sensors degrade in fog and at night. The MVP must publish its operating envelope so airport buyers can compare against their local conditions.
