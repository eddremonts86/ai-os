_Based on source brief:_ Something esoteric I happened across this week that I thought was fascinating: in a book from 1968 (https:&#x2F;&#x2F;en.wikipedia.org&#x2F;wiki&#x2F;Man,_Play_and_Games), it&#x27;s suggested that all forms of play combine 1 or more of 4 forms, each with names that sound like RPG stats:<p>Agon - Games where the players have equal chances and compete to win based on skill, strength, etc.<p>Alea - Games that depend on chance, like dice rolls, card draws, etc.<p>Mimicry - Roleplaying, storytelling etc.<p>Ilinx - Something like &quot;physical disorientation&quot;, like swings, rollercoasters, skiing, etc.<p>It also defines games as fitting somewhere on an axis between &quot;Ludus&quot; and &quot;Paidia&quot;, where Ludus has strictly-defined rules and Paidia is more spontaneous.<p>I&#x27;m not sure it really nails it - I feel like there are plenty of nuances that don&#x27;t fit neatly into these categories, but it got me to thinking about where different games sit on the Ludus&gt;Paidia axis and how they combine the 4 aspects.<p>For example, I don&#x27;t like D&amp;D so much, I like games like Dungeon World and Monster of the Week much better. On the Ludus&gt;Paidia axis, D&amp;D would be much more towards Ludus and maybe that&#x27;s a simple explanation - there&#x27;s a point on that axis that is ideal for me, while others have a different preference. I guess it&#x27;s what&#x27;s often referred to as &quot;crunchiness&quot; by a different name, but maybe the other 4 metrics offer a more interesting classification.<p>I&#x27;d say that D&amp;D runs relatively high on Agon and Alea, with strictly-defined combat rules and a D20 offering a large random range, but Mimicry is somewhat incidental to the system. Meanwhile the &quot;PBTA&quot; genre of games is much lower on Agon, not really trying to offer strategic wargame combat, and I&#x27;d argue lower on Alea due to the narrower dice roll range of 2D6, but that makes much more room for Mimicry.<p>...and I&#x27;m wondering if LARP would qualify as including Ilinx if it involves physical combat.<p>Then there&#x27;s video games, that typically combine Agon, Alea and Mimicry in one way or another, but I&#x27;ve wondered if physics-based games like Surgeon Simulator, QWOP, Human Fall Flat etc. would qualify as including an element of Ilinx (and if this is why I don&#x27;t like them).<p>There isn&#x27;t really a question here; although I&#x27;m tempted to try to create a numerical classification system, I can&#x27;t think of solid metrics to classify against other than &quot;vibes&quot;. It is interesting to think about where different games fall comparatively in these metrics, though. Was &quot;Into The Breach&quot; so popular because it provided pure Agon without the Alea? Are slot machines popular because they provide pure Alea (arguably with a side of Ilinx)? ...and then we&#x27;re crossing into human psychology and asking why we enjoy these particular things.
