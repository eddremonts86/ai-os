# The systemic crisis of athlete transition

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

An athlete leaving competition has years of evidence that they can work, be coached, perform under observation and recover from measurable failure — and no way to show it to anyone outside sport. This workspace does the translation, connects them to someone from their own sport who already crossed over, and holds a plan against their real exit date.

The capture calls this crisis systemic rather than personal, and the product takes that literally: the exit is predictable, so the preparation can start before it lands rather than after.

**One-liner:** A transition workspace that turns a competitive career into evidence an employer can read, and puts the athlete in front of someone who has already made the same move.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Athletes in their final seasons | The exit date is known and nothing in their calendar prepares for it. |
| Athletes out through injury | The transition arrived without warning and the usual timeline advice does not apply. |
| Former athletes now established | Willing to be the call the next one makes, if the ask is bounded and specific. |
| Coaches and athletic staff | Watch this recur annually and have only informal help to offer. |
| Employers who value the profile | Know athletes make good hires and cannot find them, because the signal is illegible on a resume. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Describe a competitive career in terms a non-sport reader can evaluate.
2. **Functional job** — Talk to somebody from the same sport who already did this, before the exit rather than after.
3. **Functional job** — Hold a plan with real dates against a departure that is already scheduled.
4. **Emotional job** — Stop treating the end of competition as the end of being good at something.
5. **Social job** — Be seen by the next employer as someone with a record, not someone with a gap.

## Success Metrics

- **Translation completion** — share of athletes who finish a record intake and produce at least one evidence-backed claim, which is the step everything else depends on.
- **Conversation rate** — share of routing requests that become an actual conversation, measuring whether the volunteer side holds.
- **Pre-exit engagement** — proportion of users who start before their exit date rather than after, since the systemic framing only pays off if preparation is early.
- **Plan follow-through** — dated steps completed before the exit date passes.
- **Mentor retention** — share of former athletes still accepting requests after three months; if this falls the supply side is being over-asked.
- **Destination breadth** — number of distinct fields users move into, as a check that the product is not quietly funnelling everyone into coaching.

## Pricing & Monetization

The capture names no price and no payer, and the honest reading is that the person with the problem is the one least able to pay at the moment they have it. What the design does fix is the cost shape: the expensive resource here is volunteer mentor attention, not compute, so any model has to protect that supply rather than sell access to it. Institutional payers — teams, athletic departments, player associations — are the obvious direction because they already own the exit, but the source names none of them and this stays an open question rather than a plan.

## Competitive Landscape

- **General career platforms and resume tools** — plentiful and cheap, but they take a work history as input and an athlete's history is exactly what does not fit their fields.
- **Alumni and former-player networks where they exist** — the closest real substitute, and the reason the routing feature is framed as organising something that already happens informally rather than inventing it.
- **Coaching and counselling services** — address the emotional side directly, which this product deliberately hands off rather than duplicates.

The capture names no competitor, so no further comparison is claimed.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] Determine which population this is about — end of eligibility, non-renewal, or injury — because the clock and the messaging differ for each and the source names none.
- [ ] Establish whether enough former athletes will volunteer per sport to make routing useful, since an empty directory makes the product a worksheet.
- [ ] Decide the handoff boundary for mental health need, and where the product stops.
- [ ] Confirm employers can actually read the translated evidence; if they cannot, the central mechanic is unproven.
- [ ] Resolve who the institutional payer could be without giving that institution visibility into an athlete's private transition.
- [ ] Test whether athletes will engage before the exit at all, given that engaging means accepting the career is ending.
