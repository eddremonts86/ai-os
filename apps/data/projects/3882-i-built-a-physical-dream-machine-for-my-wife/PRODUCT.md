# I built a physical dream machine for my wife

## Value Proposition

A bedside object that turns spoken dreams into pictures. Hold the button, describe the dream, and the machine transcribes it, renders an illustration with an image model, shows it on the e-ink display and makes a postcard for the website. Built by its author for his wife — a single-user, personal artifact rather than a product, and honest about how it works: she describes the dream out loud, nothing reads her mind.

**One-liner:** A physical bedside device that transcribes a spoken dream, renders an illustration on e-ink and posts a dream postcard.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| The author's wife | The named first and only user: describes dreams aloud and sees them illustrated on the e-ink display. |
| Makers and hardware tinkerers | A worked example of voice capture, transcription and image generation in one physical device. |
| Dream journalers | A speaking-first alternative to typing out dream entries each morning. |

This is a personal build; the table reflects the stated user plus the audiences who would learn from the build.

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Capture a spoken dream description with a single press-and-hold button.

2. **Functional job** — Transcribe the description and render an illustration from it.

3. **Functional job** — Display the illustration on the e-ink screen and publish it as a postcard on the website.

4. **Emotional job** — Give his wife a small magic object that makes her dreams tangible every morning.

## Success Metrics

- **Capture loop:** press and hold, speak, transcribe, render — the whole flow works from one button.
- **Display quality:** the illustration appears on the e-ink display as intended.
- **Postcard output:** every captured dream produces a postcard for the website.
- **Daily use:** the machine survives regular, real use by its one user.

## Pricing & Monetization

None stated. It is a personal gift for the author's wife; the post shows no commercial intent.

## Competitive Landscape

The post names no competitors and describes no market. The category is personal AI hardware — voice assistants, smart displays and art-generating gadgets. This build sits at the cozy end of that category: a single-purpose, single-user object, with the author himself noting he only wishes he could build a mind-reading version someday.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] Hardware reliability is unproven: one device, one user, no longevity data in the capture.
- [ ] The transcription and image models run through OpenRouter, so the gadget is dependent on API availability and cost.
- [ ] E-ink refresh speed may make the illustration reveal slower than a phone screen; the tradeoff is aesthetic, not functional.
- [ ] There is no product, pricing or roadmap — the risks listed here are the risks of a hobby build being mistaken for a product.
