# I will not promote: whats the small message thats been sitting in your drafts all afternoon?

## Tech Stack

Not applicable. The post is a behavioural reflection on a writing-and-sending pattern. The only surface mentioned is a draft (email, DM, or similar — the source does not specify). Naming any specific tool, app, or platform would be invention.

## Architecture

None. The "shape" of the post is a side-by-side comparison the author has drawn in his own head and is asking the community to validate or correct:

```
Recipient can say no to me  →  rewrite loop, all-afternoon draft, words stop sounding like me
Recipient can't say no       →  seconds, no editing, sounds like me
```

The author wants to know whether other founders have the same asymmetry, and what the opposite-experience group does that closes it.

## Milestones

The post has no milestones. The implicit personal milestones the author is working toward are:

1. Name the pattern explicitly enough that he can recognise it the next time it happens (not "I'm just being careful" but "this is the freeze-on-yes loop").

3. Find at least one opposite-mode reply that describes a concrete behaviour he can try.
4. Decide on a personal rule (e.g., a timer, a minimum-edit count, or a "send the first draft" commitment) and stick to it for a week.

## Risks

- **Mis-attribution.** Replies that frame the pattern as anxiety, perfectionism, or ADHD may be true for some readers, but the author has not invited diagnosis — and assuming one without asking is over-fit.
- **Single-afternoon data.** The post is built on one day's observation (a draft that sat since morning). Generalising to "this is who I am" from one episode is fragile.
- **Recipient-side unknown.** The author does not know what the recipient would have done if the reply had gone out at 9am. Replies that claim "it didn't matter" are guessing.
- **Tool-recommendation drift.** The thread is at risk of turning into a list of writing tools. The source is asking for human behaviour, not software.
