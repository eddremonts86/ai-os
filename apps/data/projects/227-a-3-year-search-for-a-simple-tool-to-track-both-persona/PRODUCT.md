# A 3-year search for a simple tool to track both personal and business finances in one place. Nothing matches because the reports are split, the UX is heavy, or the price is wrong.

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

Stop the double-ledger tax. The first 10,000 paying users pay for the service from the recovered time in the first month.

## Target Users

US freelancers, side-hustlers, and one-person businesses who file a Schedule C and want a single view of personal + business finances without the double-entry tax.

## Jobs To Be Done

Functional: have a single finance view that treats personal and business as one system. Emotional: stop the dread of the monthly reconciliation. Social: be the person who can answer how much do I actually have in 30 seconds.

## Success Metrics

At least 10,000 active users in 18 months. Reconciliation completion rate above 80% per month. Less than 5% of users report a wrong-totalled complaint. Export-to-accountant usage above 50% per quarter.

## Competitive Landscape

Mint, YNAB, and Monarch are personal-only. QuickBooks, Wave, and Xero are business-only with limited personal. No mainstream service combines a personal and a business ledger in one view with a US-tax-relevant summary.

## Risks & Open Questions

Plaid is a single point of failure. Schedule C logic must be defensible. Bank-linking UX can lose users if the flow is slow. Tax-relevant summary must be honest about its limitations.
