# Healthcare professionals want AI for diagnosis, documentation, and patient care — but training doesn't scale and tools feel too technical. Need a simple, clinically relevant path.

> Auto-generated product brief. Review and refine before MVP scoping.

## Value Proposition

A clinician who has 15 minutes between patients opens the platform, runs through one clinical-scenario exercise (image interpretation, discharge summary, patient-friendly explanation, guideline lookup) using an AI tool inside the lesson, and earns a module certificate — at $80/month, with zero technical prerequisite, scaling the "learn by doing" outcomes the author achieves in his Brazilian in-person workshops.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Practising clinician (physician / nurse / allied health) | Curious about AI but has no time for a generic intro course; wants clinically relevant scenarios from day one and a certificate they can put on a CV. |
| Clinical administrator / residency-program director | Wants a structured AI-fluency program they can roll out across a team without staging an in-person workshop. |
| Medical school / continuing-education provider | Wants AI fluency as a CME-eligible module that fits between shifts and does not require a faculty member to teach it. |
| The author (Gilson Leal, Brazil) | Wants to scale the outcomes of his in-person workshops; willing to provide feedback and accept 1% equity in exchange. |

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Complete a clinical AI-fluency exercise (image interpretation, discharge summary, patient-friendly explanation, guideline search) inside the platform with an AI tool and walk away with a certificate.
2. **Emotional job** — Stop feeling that "AI is for tech people"; trust that the scenarios are clinically relevant because they were designed by a clinician-trainer, not a generic course platform.
3. **Social job** — Be the colleague who can say "I use AI for discharge summaries now" instead of "I'm curious about AI but haven't found the time".

## Success Metrics

- **Activation:** ≥ 70% of signups complete the first module within 7 days.
- **Exercise completion:** ≥ 80% of started modules end in a clinician performing the AI-assisted clinical task, not a multiple-choice quiz.
- **Retention:** ≥ 50% of subscribers remain active after 90 days; churn above 60% would mean the "learn by doing" promise is not landing.
- **CME submission:** ≥ 30% of completers submit the module certificate for CME credit where applicable, validating the certificate's value as an adoption lever.
- **Author feedback loop:** the Brazilian author (Gilson) reviews the top-requested module topics monthly so the curriculum tracks real clinician demand.

## Pricing & Monetization

$80/month subscription (the author's stated willingness to pay), monthly cancel, no per-module paywall. Annual lock at $65/month. Team / residency-program plans at a flat per-seat price with a shared progress dashboard. CME submission fees (where applicable) are passed through at cost with no markup.

## Competitive Landscape

- **Coursera / edX "AI for Healthcare"** — generic intro to AI courses with a healthcare tag; designed for knowledge workers, not practising clinicians; no scenario-based "learn by doing" exercises that fire between patients.
- **In-person workshops (the author's own)** — what works today; high quality, low scale, geography-bound; the platform's pitch is "same outcomes, your phone, between patients".
- **Hospital-internal AI training** — generic LMS modules built by hospital IT; usually compliance-led, not clinically relevant; clinicians skip them.
- **Vendor-led training (Epic, Nuance, Abridge, etc.)** — product-specific; teaches the clinician to use one vendor's tool, not to evaluate and use AI across the workflow.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] CME eligibility varies by jurisdiction (US ACCME, Brazilian CNA, EU EACCME); decide which jurisdictions to pursue first so the certificate is not "decorative" for the target market.
- [ ] Validate the "learn by doing" exercise model against a generic "watch a video, take a quiz" alternative — if clinicians skip the exercises, the platform is just another LMS and the $80/month price collapses.
- [ ] Confirm the author (Gilson Leal) is willing to be credited as a curriculum reviewer / clinical advisor so the "clinician-designed" claim is honest and his 1% equity proposal is documented.
- [ ] Decide whether to ship with one AI provider or expose a "bring your own API key" mode for hospital systems that have negotiated enterprise LLM contracts.
