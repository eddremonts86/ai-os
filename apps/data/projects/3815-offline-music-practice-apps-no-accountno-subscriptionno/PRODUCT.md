# Offline music practice apps – no account,no subscription,no analytics

## Value Proposition

Practice software that gets out of the way. The Buddha apps (Guitar, Ukulele, Drum, Bass) are built on the belief that practice stops the moment it becomes a game you can lose: no streaks, no badges, no accounts, no cloud, no analytics unless you opt in — and free forever with nothing hidden behind a tip jar. Each app does the quiet essentials for its instrument: learn songs, capture ideas before they fade, jam with a backing band, and for the guitar app, tune up — all living on the device, so your music stays yours. It is deliberately not a platform: one musician, no growth team, and calm as the feature.

**One-liner:** A family of offline-first instrument practice apps — no account, no subscription, no analytics, free forever.

## Target Users

| Stakeholder | Why they care |
|---|---|
| Casual instrument learners | Songs and jamming without gamification pressure. |
| Privacy-conscious musicians | Everything on-device; opt-in anonymous analytics only. |
| Guitarists specifically | Riff capture, backing band and tuner in the original app. |
| Android users (soon) | Named as coming, gated on a one-email notification list. |

The site states no commercial market; it presents a personal, philosophy-first product line.

## Jobs To Be Done

1. **Functional job** — Learn a song on the instrument, screen by screen.
2. **Functional job** — Capture a riff or idea the moment it appears, before it fades.
3. **Functional job** — Jam along with a backing band and tune the instrument.
4. **Emotional job** — Practice without streaks, guilt or surveillance, knowing nothing is tracking you.

## Success Metrics

- **Sustained use:** sessions per week per instrument app, with no streak mechanics to inflate or shame it.
- **Tip jar support:** share of active users who tip — the only stated revenue signal.
- **Idea capture:** riffs saved per week (the "capture an idea" promise).
- **Android launch:** the waitlist converts into the promised single email and the Android build ships.
- **Privacy posture:** zero support or review complaints about data practices, matching the no-analytics promise.

## Pricing & Monetization

The site is explicit: free means free — every feature in every app, forever, with an optional tip jar that unlocks nothing. There is no subscription and no other monetization stated.

## Competitive Landscape

The post does not name competitors. The category is music practice apps (from gamified streak-based tutors to subscription lesson platforms); the Buddha apps' position is the anti-gamification niche — offline-first, account-free, ad-free practice utilities whose stated rivals are, implicitly, every app that pressures users with streaks and paywalls.

## Risks & Open Questions

- [ ] Free forever with only a tip jar is a stated promise; if costs rise, the business model has no other lever.
- [ ] A solo developer shipping four apps plus an Android port stretches QA and platform support thin.
- [ ] No sync by design means users with multiple devices get multiple, separate practice histories.
- [ ] "Quietly remembers what you've practiced" needs care to not become the very tracking the philosophy rejects.
- [ ] The capture names no app-store history, ratings or revenue; adoption is unverified.
