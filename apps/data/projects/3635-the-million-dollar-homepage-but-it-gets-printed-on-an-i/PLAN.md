# The Million Dollar Homepage, but it gets printed on an iPhone skin

## Tech Stack

- **Next.js 14 (App Router)** for the storefront and the contributor account surface, because the grid interaction needs a fast client experience and a server-rendered catalog page.
- **TypeScript** end to end, because tile selection, payment status and certificate generation have multiple interacting types.
- **Stripe Checkout** for payments at US$1 per tile and US$200 for the camera-area block, with the webhook driving the tile-lock transition.
- **Sharp** for the server-side image processing pipeline that resizes and pads uploads to the 2.8 by 2.8 mm tile specification and validates the solid background.
- **PostgreSQL** for the persistent store of tiles, payments, processed images and contributor records, with a unique constraint on (grid_x, grid_y) to prevent double-locking.
- **S3-compatible object storage** for the processed tile images and the final composite, because the storage is per-object and the size of the composite benefits from object-store characteristics.
- **Coolify** for deployment, matching the storefront's hosting shape.
- **A scheduled job** that locks the grid permanently at the October 1, 2026 close and produces the final composite image.

## Architecture

A contributor lands on the storefront and sees the 25 × 45 grid with each tile showing availability. Selecting a tile opens a panel where the contributor picks a tile pack (a contiguous block), uploads a JPG or JPEG of 10 MB or less with a solid background, and sees a preview of the processed tile on the grid. The preview is generated by the same Sharp pipeline that will produce the final tile, so what the contributor sees is what will be saved.

Payment is Stripe Checkout at US$1 per tile or US$200 for the optional camera-area block. The Stripe webhook drives the lock transition: on payment success, the chosen tiles are marked as sold, the processed images are persisted to object storage, and the contributor record is written with the chosen tiles and coordinates. The Stripe webhook is the source of truth for the lock — the contributor never edits a paid tile.

The contributor account surface lists the contributor's tiles, allows downloading the personal-use image once it is available, and generates a certificate carrying the contributor name, tile count and coordinates. The certificate is generated at lock time and refreshed when the final composite is produced.

The submissions-close job runs at the deadline (October 1, 2026) and closes the grid permanently. It produces the final composite image by composing the 1,116 processed tiles into a single picture sized to the iPhone 17 Pro Max skin area. The handoff to the project creator for printing is whatever shape the source page describes — the plan does not invent a print partner or a shipping arrangement.

The non-affiliation statement with Apple Inc. is rendered in the storefront footer, on every receipt and on every downloadable artefact, because the trademark line is part of the stated contract with the audience.

## Milestones

1. **M1 — Grid and selection** — the 25 × 45 grid component, the per-tile state model and the pack-selection flow.
2. **M2 — Upload and preview** — the JPG / JPEG upload with the 10 MB ceiling, the solid-background check, and the Sharp preview render.
3. **M3 — Stripe Checkout** — the per-tile and US$200 camera-area flows, with the webhook driving the tile-lock transition.
4. **M4 — Lock enforcement** — server-side immutability of paid tiles, with the unique-constraint guarantee on (grid_x, grid_y).
5. **M5 — Contributor surface** — the account page, the final-image download and the certificate generation.
6. **M6 — Submissions-close job** — the October 1, 2026 deadline job that locks the grid and produces the final composite.
7. **M7 — Trademark audit** — CI that asserts the Apple non-affiliation statement is present on every public surface and every deliverable.
8. **M8 — Print handoff** — whichever delivery shape the source page describes for the iPhone 17 Pro Max skin, with the print partner named if the source names one.

## Risks

- **Grid not filling** — an empty grid changes the deliverable shape; the close-time behaviour has to be defined before October 1, 2026.
- **Stripe webhook race conditions** — the lock transition depends on the webhook; a missed or out-of-order webhook can leave a tile unpaid-but-shown-sold.
- **Solid-background false positives/negatives** — the background check is the only quality gate the source names; a strict check rejects too many, a lenient one rejects too few.
- **Print partner un-named** — the source does not name a print partner, and inventing one would be a fabrication; the handoff is an open question flagged in the risks.
- **Refund policy enforcement** — the policy is narrower than typical consumer-protection norms, and a contributor dispute can require an explicit policy surface.
- **Trademark drift** — a single deliverable that drops the Apple non-affiliation line breaks the contract; CI has to enforce it.
- **Image immutability after payment** — a contributor who wants to "fix" a paid tile has to be told no, every time, and the data layer has to make "no" the only option.
