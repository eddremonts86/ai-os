# Landing ↔ product continuity

A high-quality landing page reduces uncertainty and shows the product. It does not rely on abstract
phrases or generic illustration to hide what the thing does.

The failure this skill prevents: a landing that promises one outcome and a product whose first screen
talks about something else. That break costs more conversions than any visual detail.

## The hero must answer five questions

1. Who is it for?
2. What outcome does it produce?
3. Why is it different?
4. What should the visitor do now?
5. What will they see when they get in?

If the hero cannot answer all five, no amount of visual craft fixes it.

## Page structure

1. Audience + outcome + differentiation
2. A CTA matched to the level of commitment being asked
3. A real screenshot or demonstration of the product
4. Proof placed next to each important claim — not all pooled at the bottom
5. The main flow explained in a few steps
6. Use cases by job or segment
7. Results and evidence
8. Integrations or relevant requirements
9. Objections handled: security, migration, support, price
10. A final CTA consistent with the first

## Avoid

- "Revolutionise your business with AI" with no stated outcome
- Screenshots too small or too low-contrast to read
- Mockups that do not match the real product
- Endless feature lists
- Customer logos with no context
- Generic testimonials
- Metrics with no source
- A high-commitment CTA before the value is explained
- A promise on the landing that disappears during onboarding

## The continuity test

Run it as an actual walk-through, not a thought experiment:

1. Write down the exact sentence in the hero that would convince the target user.
2. Sign up as a new user.
3. Read the first message inside the product.
4. **Do they continue the same thought?** If the hero promises "find the risk before it hits your
   business" and the first screen says "Welcome! Complete your profile", the promise broke.
5. Check the CTA mapping: each landing CTA should land in the onboarding path that delivers *that*
   specific promise — not all of them into the same generic signup.

Record the break as a P0 finding when it exists: it invalidates the acquisition spend upstream of it.

## Vocabulary alignment

Marketing and product must share the same words for the same things. Build a two-column list — landing
term vs in-product term — and reconcile it. Every mismatch is a support ticket waiting to happen.

## Checklist

- [ ] The hero answers all five questions
- [ ] A real, legible product screenshot or demo appears above the fold or immediately after
- [ ] Every important claim has proof next to it
- [ ] Metrics cite a source
- [ ] The CTA matches the commitment level and routes to the matching onboarding
- [ ] The first in-product message continues the hero sentence
- [ ] Landing and product use the same terminology
- [ ] Objections (security, migration, support, price) are addressed
- [ ] No mockup shows a product that does not exist
