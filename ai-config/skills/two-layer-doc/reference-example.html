Technical notes: not needed for the meeting

For people who want to know how each change would be built. Nothing below needs a decision today.

1 · The split: how we would build it

  • Data does not change. Recipients keep sharePct, deceased and inheritedFromId (heir points to the parent). No migration.
  • Wizard. Step 4 hides "Mark deceased" and "Add heir". "Split share" is a row action: it divides one author's share between co-authors and keeps the group at 100 %.
  • Rule. heirsBalanced in server/src/domain/approvalValidation.ts leaves the approval gate (code heirs_sum_mismatch) and joins the payout checks next to payment_recipient_deceased in domain/shared/payments.ts, which already blocks paying a deceased person.
  • After signing. Heirs are entered in the locked contract's payout section, the one part that stays editable. Each save is a history event. Schilling's deceased date pre-fills the row, as the step 4 hint already does.
  • Our assumption, to confirm. Heirs change who is paid, not the signed text: the document keeps the original author.
  • Tests. The heirs unit test in approvalValidation.test.ts moves to the payout rule; one browser test covers split at creation, then heirs after signing.

2 · Left to distribute: how we would build it

  • Frontend only. Per group: left = 100 − the sum of sharePct of active royalty recipients, with heirs counted in their parent's group. It is the same sum royaltyGroupsBalanced already uses, rounded to two decimals with the server's 0.005 tolerance.
  • Where. The group total row in step 4 shows the counter and replaces the "must sum to 100 %" error while typing. The server rule share_sum_not_100 stays as the safety net, so nothing can be approved off by a cent.
  • Share ladders. The counter reads the share at volume zero. Every other print run still goes through shareBalanceIssues (share_ladder_unbalanced).
  • If the original author is the balance. Their sharePct is computed as 100 minus the others, shown read-only, and still stored. Schilling's write and the documents see the same numbers as today. This is the case that depends on how the DB holds the share.
  • Test. One unit test on the counter: 50 + 35 leaves 15; a full group leaves 0; three shares of 33.33 leave 0.01.

3 · AI rights: how we would build it

  • Today. coverage.aiRights holds two booleans, trainingAllowed and generativeDerivation. They show in step 3, the summary, the history labels, the terms-template defaults and the document preview. They are not sent to Schilling: no write in server/src/domain/schillingWrites/ reads them.
  • Small. No data change. Step 3 draws the switches from one short list of uses instead of two hand-written rows, so a third use is one new entry and one field.
  • Large. The eight uses become rights formats, the way Schilling's plan models them. KM's format checklist (coverage.formatCodes) and rate table (royaltyRates, step 5) already carry formats with rates, so no new field is needed. It waits for the eight formats to exist in Schilling's catalogue.
  • Moving from small to large. One migration maps the two booleans to two format codes. Then fieldLabels, fieldDiff and the template defaults change with it.
  • Effort. Small touches a few frontend files. Large is mostly Schilling's work first, then catalogue entries and templates on our side.
